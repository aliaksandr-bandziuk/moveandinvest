## 0. The one thing that decides whether this page is worth publishing

The competitor study found the market's blind spot, and it is a single fact.

**Of twelve pages examined across six searches, none mentions that an
investment-permit applicant is still subject to the means-of-subsistence test.**
Not Immigrant Invest, which cites art. 90-A by number and stops short of saying
what it disapplies. Not Get Golden Visa across 12,500 words and 32 questions.
Not Portugalist. The space is empty for a commercial reason: every publisher
ranking on these terms sells the service, and "your €500,000 does not exempt you
from proving income" loses deals.

**The verification that makes this publishable was done on 28 August 2026** and
is recorded in the cost-of-living file: art. 90-A(1)(a) was read in the
consolidated text of Lei 23/2007, **19th version, updated to Lei n.º 61/2025 de
22/10**, in the PGD Lisboa database, and art. 77(1) in the original Diário da
República PDF of 4 July 2007. The exception is singular — "com exceção da alínea
a) do n.º 1" — and alínea d), means of subsistence, survives.

**The article must state which consolidated version was read and on what date.**
That sentence is the difference between a claim and a checkable claim, and no

## 1. The residence visa, and why there is no "D7 article"

**There is no article of Lei 23/2007 dedicated to the D7.** The route rests on
the general residence visa in **art. 58**, plus the general permit conditions in
art. 77 and the Portaria 1563/2007 scale. Arts. 59–64 cover the *specific* visas
— subordinate work, independent work, research, study, student mobility, family
reunification — and none of them covers a holder of own income.

Art. 58, verbatim from the original DR text:

> "1 — O visto de residência destina-se a permitir ao seu titular a entrada em
> território português a fim de solicitar autorização de residência. 2 — O visto
> de residência é válido para duas entradas em território português e habilita o
> seu titular a nele permanecer por um período de quatro meses. […] 4 — Sem
> prejuízo de prazos mais curtos previstos nesta lei, o prazo para a decisão
> sobre o pedido de visto de residência é de 60 dias."

https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2007/07/12700/42904330.pdf

So: two entries, four months in Portugal, and a 60-day statutory decision on the
visa. Art. 58 is **not** in the amendment list of Lei 61/2025.

**Consular fee: €110.** Portaria n.º 91/2025/1, de 10 de março, amending the
consular emoluments table of Portaria n.º 229/2021: "Vistos nacionais 110,00 €".
In force from 11 March 2025.
https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2025/03/04800/0000900017.pdf
*Caveat: this is the single "national visas" line. The consolidated table on DRE
is JavaScript-gated and could not be opened to rule out a D7-specific line.*

**Where the application is filed — NOT FOUND IN A PRIMARY SOURCE.** Whether it
goes to the consulate directly or through VFS Global is stated only on
vistos.mne.gov.pt, the consulate sites and gov.pt, all unreachable across this
research. **The article must not assert VFS.**

### The AIMA appointment is booked before you fly, and its deadline is the visa

This is worth stating precisely because the secondary guides get it wrong in a
specific way: they publish a flat "120 days" as though it were a rule of its own.

**Decreto Regulamentar n.º 84/2007, art. 14.º**, read at PGD Lisboa:

> n.º 1: "Dos pareceres positivos relativos a vistos de residência, emitidos pela
> AIMA, I. P., […] consta, sempre que no pedido for indicada a data da viagem, o
> agendamento para apresentação na AIMA, I. P., do interessado […]"
> n.º 2: "O agendamento previsto no número anterior deve respeitar o prazo de
> validade do respetivo visto de residência."

https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=940&tabela=leis

The appointment is included in AIMA's favourable opinion when the applicant
states a travel date, and it must fall inside the visa's own validity — the four
months of art. 58(2). It is not a separate 120-day clock.

## 2. Validity and renewal

**Art. 75.º, n.º 1:** the temporary permit is valid for **two years** from the
issue of the title and renews for **successive three-year periods**.

> "Sem prejuízo das disposições legais especiais aplicáveis, a autorização de
> residência temporária é válida pelo período de dois anos contados a partir da
> data da emissão do respetivo título e é renovável por períodos sucessivos de
> três anos."

**Source status: SECONDARY for the wording.** The current text was reachable only
on LEGISPÉDIA, a Google Sites compilation. DRE is JavaScript-gated and PGD
Lisboa truncates this law around art. 31. **Primary corroboration that it stands:**
Lei 61/2025's re-enactment of art. 75 renders n.º 1 as unchanged and rewrites
only n.º 2.

**Neither 2025 law touched these periods.**

- **Lei n.º 55-C/2025, de 22 de julho** creates the Unidade Nacional de
  Estrangeiros e Fronteiras. Its only contact with Lei 23/2007 is art. 9.º, a
  renaming provision.
- **Lei n.º 61/2025, de 22 de outubro** amends arts. 45, 46, 52, 52-A, 57-A, 75,
  87-A, 89, 98, 99, 101, 103, 104, 105, 106, 122, 123 and adds 87-B. Its change
  to art. 75 is confined to n.º 2, tightening the CPLP route so the applicant
  must hold a residence visa rather than a short-stay visa or lawful entry. In
  force 23 October 2025.
  https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2025/10/20400/0000900017.pdf

*Do not write that the periods "changed from 1+2 to 2+3" as a dated event: the
original 2007 text was one year renewable for two, neither Lei 29/2012 nor Lei
102/2017 amended art. 75, and the amending instrument could not be identified.
State the current rule, not its history.*

## 3. Permanent residence at five years

**Art. 80.º of Lei 23/2007**, verbatim from the original DR PDF, and not in Lei
61/2025's amendment list:

> "1 — […] beneficiam de uma autorização de residência permanente os cidadãos
> estrangeiros que, cumulativamente: a) Sejam titulares de autorização de
> residência temporária há pelo menos cinco anos; b) Durante os últimos cinco
> anos […] não tenham sido condenados em pena ou penas que, isolada ou
> cumulativamente, ultrapassem 1 ano de prisão; c) Disponham de meios de
> subsistência, tal como definidos pela portaria a que se refere a alínea d) do
> n.º 1 do artigo 52.º; d) Disponham de alojamento; e) Comprovem ter conhecimento
> do Português básico."

**Art. 76.º, n.º 1:** "A autorização de residência permanente não tem limite de
validade" — the permit does not expire; the card is renewed every five years.

**The language level is not in the statute.** Art. 80(1)(e) says "Português
básico" and nothing more. The A2 equivalence everyone publishes is
regulation-level, and AIMA's own page stating it was unreachable. **The article
must not print A2 as statutory.**

Note for the piece: art. 80(1)(c) applies the same means-of-subsistence test at
the five-year mark. The income question does not go away when the permit does.

## 4. Citizenship — the law in force today

**Enacted, in force, not pending and not struck down.**

**Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18 de maio**, amending and republishing Lei 37/81.
https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2026/05/09500/0000200020.pdf

**Commencement, art. 8.º:** "A presente lei entra em vigor no dia seguinte ao da
sua publicação" — **in force since 19 May 2026**.

**Qualifying period, art. 6.º n.º 1 al. b):**

> "Residirem legalmente no território português há pelo menos sete anos, no caso
> de nacionais de países de língua oficial portuguesa e de cidadãos de
> Estados-Membros da União Europeia, ou 10 anos, no caso de nacionais de outros
> países"

**Language, culture and civics, art. 6.º n.º 1 al. c), d), e):**

> c) "Comprovarem, através de teste ou de certificado, conhecer suficientemente a
> língua e a cultura portuguesas, a história e os símbolos nacionais"
> d) "Conhecerem suficientemente os direitos e deveres fundamentais inerentes à
> nacionalidade portuguesa e a organização política do Estado português"
> e) "Declararem solenemente a sua adesão aos princípios fundamentais do Estado
> de direito democrático"

The culture, history and symbols limb and the solemn declaration are new. **No
CEFR level appears in this statute either** — same caution as art. 80.

**CPLP presumption, art. 6.º n.º 10:** nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries
are presumed to satisfy the language limb unless a lack of Portuguese is manifest.

**How residence is counted, art. 15.º:** n.º 1 counts anyone lawfully present
under any title, visa or authorisation of the immigration regime; n.º 3
aggregates broken periods only within a window of 6, 9 or 12 years for stateless
persons, CPLP and EU citizens, and everyone else respectively.

**The transitional rule, and it is the sentence that matters most to a reader —
art. 7.º:**

> "2 — Aos procedimentos administrativos pendentes à data da entrada em vigor da
> presente lei aplica-se a Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, na redação anterior à
> presente lei."

Applications **filed before 19 May 2026 are decided under the old five-year
regime**. Filed on or after that date, the new periods apply.

**What the Constitutional Court struck down, and what it did not.** Acórdão n.º
1133/2025 of 15 December 2025 (Processo 1383/2025) pronounced four norms of the
*earlier* decree unconstitutional: art. 6.º n.º 1 al. f) (automatic bar on
convictions over two years), art. 12.º-B n.º 3, art. 7.º n.ºs 3 and 4
(requirements assessed at petition rather than decision date), and by majority
art. 9.º n.º 1 al. a) (loss of nationality for conduct rejecting adhesion to the
national community). **The seven- and ten-year periods were not among them.** The
President then vetoed and returned the text; the revised text became LO 1/2026.

**Source status for the Acórdão: SECONDARY.** tribunalconstitucional.pt was
unreachable; the official TC PDF was read from a mirror hosted by Poder360. **Do
not quote the Acórdão verbatim without re-reading it at the court's own domain.**

**Do not conflate** LO 1/2026 with the separate diploma creating an accessory
penalty of loss of nationality, which was vetoed for unconstitutionality in May
2026 and is not in force. That is secondary reporting (Observador, Diário de
Aveiro, 12 May 2026) and belongs in the article only as a named absence, if at
all.

## 5. AIMA capacity and the statutory clock

**Statutory deadlines, art. 82.º of Lei 23/2007:** 60 days to decide a grant, 30
days to decide a renewal, with **tacit approval on renewal** where the delay is
not attributable to the applicant. Read from the original 2007 DR PDF; art. 82 is
not in Lei 61/2025's amendment list, but the consolidated text could not be
opened. **Treat as very likely current rather than verified.**

**Backlog: 30,000 files awaiting a grant or refusal decision as at 1 July 2026.**
Stated by Secretary of State Rui Armindo Freitas at a hearing of the Assembleia
da República's Comissão de Assuntos Constitucionais: "os únicos processos ainda
que estão pendentes na Estrutura de Missão são 30 mil pendentes de deferimento ou
indeferimento." The AIMA Estrutura de Missão itself closed at the end of December
2025.

**Source status: SECONDARY.** A parliamentary statement carried by Lusa and
printed by Diário de Leiria; AIMA's own publication and the committee record were
unreachable. **Attribute it to the Secretary of State and the date, not to AIMA.**

## 6. Tax

**The 20% regime is IFICI**, created by **art. 58.º-A of the Estatuto dos
Benefícios Fiscais** and regulated by **Portaria n.º 352/2024/1, de 23 de
dezembro**. https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2024/12/24800/0004000045.pdf

**Qualifying activities** are listed in the annex by CPP occupational code: 112
company directors and executives; 12 administrative and commercial services
directors; 13 production and specialised services directors; 21 specialists in
physical sciences, mathematics and engineering; 221 doctors; 231 university and
higher-education teachers; 25 information and communication technology
specialists.

**Registration deadline, art. 2.º:** "até 15 de janeiro do ano seguinte àquele em
que se tornem residentes". Confirmed independently on the tax authority's own
FAQ, which also gives the rate — a special rate of 20% on category A and B income
from qualifying activity — and the duration of ten consecutive years.
https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/questoes_frequentes/pages/faqs-01018.aspx

**Ordinary tax residence, CIRS art. 16.º:**

> n.º 1 al. a): "Hajam nele permanecido mais de 183 dias, seguidos ou
> interpolados, em qualquer período de 12 meses com início ou fim no ano em causa"
> n.º 2: "considera-se como dia de presença em território português qualquer dia,
> completo ou parcial, que inclua dormida no mesmo."

https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/cirs_rep/Pages/irs16.aspx

Two drafting points worth the article's space, because both are commonly
misstated: the window is a **rolling twelve months** and not the calendar year,
and **any day including an overnight stay counts**, whole or partial. Al. b)
makes a habitual abode an independent alternative test for those present fewer
days.

**Already verified in the earlier file and reusable:** the pensions exclusion —
category H income and income from blacklisted jurisdictions are outside the
regime and taxed at 35%.

## 7. Family reunification

**The headline change of 2025: the sponsor must have held the permit for two
years.** Art. 98.º n.º 1 as amended by Lei 61/2025:

> "O titular de autorização de residência válida há pelo menos dois anos tem
> direito ao reagrupamento familiar com os membros da família abrangidos pelos
> artigos 99.º e 100.º […]"

Exemptions survive for dependent minors and incapacitated persons and for holders
under arts. 90.º, 90.º-A and 121.º-A, with a carve-out for spouses who cohabited
for fifteen months before entry. Confirmed primary from the Lei 61/2025 DR PDF.

**Who qualifies, art. 99.º n.º 1:** spouse; minor or incapacitated children
dependent on the couple or on one spouse; adopted minors; adult unmarried
dependent children studying in Portugal; adult unmarried studying children where
the sponsor holds an investment permit; dependent first-degree ascendants in the
direct line; minor siblings under the sponsor's guardianship. Lei 61/2025 added
n.º 6, requiring the marriage to be valid and recognised under Portuguese law and
setting a minimum age of 18. **The enumeration is SECONDARY (LEGISPÉDIA); the
n.º 6 addition is primary from the Lei 61/2025 PDF.**

**Does the income requirement rise per person? Yes, and the statute now says so —
art. 101.º as amended by Lei 61/2025:**

> "Meios de subsistência suficientes para sustentar todos os membros do
> agrupamento familiar, sem recurso a apoios sociais"

The amount is not set here; it is the Portaria 1563/2007 scale applied across the
household. The "sem recurso a apoios sociais" clause is new emphasis and worth
quoting.

## 10. A note on the Polish version

The keyword study found something that changes what the Polish localisation can
be: **Poles are EU citizens, and the D7 and D8 do not apply to them.** A Pole
moving to Portugal registers as an EU citizen and obtains a CRUE at the câmara
municipal — no visa, no income threshold, no AIMA visa file.

The Polish results for these terms are dominated by CRUE explainers and EU
free-movement pages, and the pages ranking for Polish D7 queries are
machine-translated; one ranking page had every heading in English.

So the Polish version cannot be a translation of this guide. Two honest options:
scope it to the genuine Polish audience — a Pole with a non-EU spouse or non-EU
parents, researching that person's permit — or say plainly at the top that a
Polish passport holder does not need any of this, and make the guide about what
they do need. The second is the version the site's own method points to, and it
is the one the cost-of-living article already used.

Deciding this is not a research question and is left to the brief.
