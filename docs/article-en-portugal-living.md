## Article

Ask what Portugal costs and you will be given a monthly budget. The page ranking first for this question at the time of writing is a sample budget for a couple, written by a resident, with no statistic behind any line of it. The second-largest result is a money-transfer company's marketing page: one table, no source. A crowdsourced index sits between them and at least says that it is crowdsourced.

Portugal's statistical office, the Instituto Nacional de Estatística, publishes something better and almost nobody uses it: **median rent per square metre by municipality, computed from contracts that were actually signed**, and median purchase prices on the same geography. This page is those releases, with the date each was published and the date it was read.

Where a figure does not exist, it is named as missing rather than filled in.

### Rent in Portugal: €9.46 a square metre, from 39,395 signed contracts

In the first quarter of 2026 the median rent in new residential leases across Portugal was **€9.46 per square metre**, up **9.1%** on the same quarter a year earlier — an acceleration, since the previous quarter had shown 7.9%.

The number rests on **39,395 new contracts**, itself up 0.7% year on year. That is the sentence worth pausing on: this is not an average of listings, and it is not a survey of what people say they pay. It is the price at which new leases were signed and registered.

| Setúbal Peninsula | €11.35 /m² |

| Porto Metropolitan Area | €10.13 /m² |
| **Portugal** | **€9.46 /m²** |

**The rise is universal, and that is a finding rather than a summary.** The median increased in every NUTS III subregion and in all twenty-four municipalities of more than a hundred thousand inhabitants. There is no cheap corner that held still. The steepest annual increase among those large municipalities was in Vila Nova de Famalicão, at 15.1%.

#### Cost of living in Lisbon: the rent alone exceeds the minimum wage

A median is a rate, not a bill, so here is the arithmetic and it is presented as arithmetic. A seventy-square-metre flat at the Lisbon municipal median comes to about **€1,219 a month**. The same flat at the Greater Lisbon median is about €1,007, and at the national median about €662.

Set the first of those against the national minimum wage, which is **€920 a month** in 2026. In the capital, the median rent on an ordinary flat is above the full monthly minimum wage before anything is eaten, heated or insured.

This is not a claim about what any particular flat costs. It is what the state's own median implies at a common size, and it is the calculation a reader can check.

#### Cost of living in Porto: €10.13, and the gap to Lisbon is the story

Porto's metropolitan median is **€10.13 per square metre** — about 42% below the municipality of Lisbon, and the lowest of the six areas in the table above. The seventy-metre arithmetic gives roughly €709 a month.

The Porto discount is real and it is the reason the city appears in every relocation article. What those articles rarely add is that Porto's median rose too, in the same universal increase, and that the comparison worth making is not Porto against Lisbon today but Porto against Porto a year ago.

#### The Algarve, Madeira and the Setúbal peninsula

Madeira at **€11.97** is the second most expensive area in the country to rent, above the Setúbal peninsula and above the Algarve. That ordering surprises people who expect the holiday coast to lead and an Atlantic island to trail.

The Algarve at **€10.71** and Setúbal at **€11.35** sit between Porto and Greater Lisbon. Setúbal in particular is the commuter answer to Lisbon prices, and at €11.35 against Lisbon's €17.42 it is a discount rather than an escape.

### Buying instead of renting: €2,198 a square metre, rising 17.5% a year

In the fourth quarter of 2025 the median price of housing sold in Portugal was **€2,198 per square metre**, up **17.5%** year on year — again an acceleration, against 16.1% the quarter before. For the whole of 2025 the median was €2,076.

The volume moved the other way: **41,789 transactions, down 5.3%** year on year. Fewer sales at sharply higher prices is the shape of a market where supply, not demand, is the binding constraint.

Prices rose in twenty-four of the twenty-six NUTS III subregions. Two fell: Alto Tâmega e Barroso by 12.1% and Madeira by 8.3% — so Madeira is simultaneously the second most expensive place to rent and one of only two places where the purchase price came down.

### The 49% nobody publishes: what a foreign buyer pays per square metre

This is the figure that repaid going to the source, and we have found it nowhere else in English.

**INE computes the median price separately according to the buyer's tax domicile.** In the fourth quarter of 2025 the median price per square metre paid by buyers domiciled abroad exceeded the median paid by buyers domiciled in Portugal by:

- **49.0% in Greater Lisbon**
- **35.6% in the Porto Metropolitan Area**

![2]

**What can and cannot be said about this.** The difference is measured by the national statistical office, so the difference itself is not in doubt. The cause is not stated anywhere in the release, and we are not going to supply one. A different segment of the market, different neighbourhoods, different urgency, different willingness to negotiate in a second language — all are plausible and none is established. Anyone telling you which it is, is guessing.

What it does mean practically is that the regional medians in the table above are not the price a foreign buyer should expect to pay in Lisbon or Porto. They are the midpoint of a market in which one identifiable group pays measurably more.

The release does not publish the absolute price for each domicile group, only the difference between them. That is why the figure above is drawn as an index rather than in euros.

### What Portugal costs against the rest of the EU

The one measure that is computed by a single office on a single method for every member state is Eurostat's **comparative price level index** for household final consumption, where the EU average is 100.

Portugal stands at **86.6** on the 2025 round — roughly 13% below the EU average price level. Eurostat flags the 2025 figures as preliminary and subject to revision when the calculation completes, so the number may move.

For context inside this site's other jurisdictions: Greece is at 86 on the 2024 round, Malta at 91.9 on the 2025 preliminary round.

### The number we will not print, and why

Every page on this subject eventually quotes an average household's total annual spending. For Portugal the figure in circulation is €24,190 a year, attributed to the 2022/2023 household budget survey.

**We could not confirm it, and on 7 September 2026 we tried again and failed.** The statistical office's portal searches fine — the rent and price releases above were read there — but a search for the household expenditure survey returns press releases for the 2010/2011 and 2005/2006 waves only. Searches for the 2022/2023 wave and for the indicator's own name return nothing. The Eurostat household budget table is served by JavaScript and returned no Portuguese rows; the national data portal does not carry the indicator.

So the figure may well be right. We have not seen it in the source, we cannot tell you when it was published, and a page whose argument is that numbers should be traceable does not get to make an exception for a convenient one. It is not on this page.

What stands in its place is what is verifiable: the rent and purchase medians above, the Eurostat price level, and the statutory figures below.

### What the law says you need: €920 a month

Two numbers govern the paperwork rather than the shopping.

The **national minimum wage is €920 a month** in 2026, set by article 3 of Decreto-Lei 139/2025 of 29 December, in force from 1 January. It is the anchor for the means-of-subsistence test on Portuguese residence permits: 100% for the main applicant, 50% for a second adult and 30% per child, under article 2(2) of Portaria 1563/2007.

The **IAS**, the social support index used across Portuguese administrative law, is **€537.13**. It is not the residence threshold, and the two are confused constantly.

Put beside the rent figures, the arithmetic is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly: the income Portugal requires a resident to prove is below the median rent on a seventy-square-metre flat in its capital.

### Frequently asked questions

? What is the average rent in Portugal?
The state does not publish an average; it publishes a median, which is more useful. In the first quarter of 2026 the median rent in new leases was €9.46 per square metre nationally and €17.42 in the municipality of Lisbon. At seventy square metres that is roughly €662 and €1,219 a month respectively.

? Is Portugal still cheap?
Against the EU average price level, yes: Portugal is at 86.6 where the EU is 100, about 13% below. Against Portugal a year earlier, no: median rent rose 9.1% and median purchase prices 17.5%, and rent rose in every subregion and every large municipality without exception.

? How much does it cost to live in Lisbon compared to Porto?
On rent, the municipality of Lisbon's median is €17.42 per square metre against €10.13 in the Porto metropolitan area — Porto is about 42% lower. On purchase, Lisbon municipality is €5,198 per square metre against €2,305 for the Porto metropolitan area.

? Where is the cheapest place to live in Portugal?
The published table does not answer that directly: it names the expensive areas, and the national median of €9.46 sits well below all six of them, which means the cheaper half of the country is outside the metropolitan areas. Every NUTS III subregion saw its median rise, so nowhere held still.

? Do foreigners pay more for property in Portugal?
Buyers with a tax domicile abroad paid a median price per square metre 49.0% higher in Greater Lisbon and 35.6% higher in the Porto metropolitan area in the fourth quarter of 2025. That difference is measured by the statistical office. Its cause is not stated, and anyone who tells you the cause is guessing.

? How much money do I need to move to Portugal?
For the paperwork, the means-of-subsistence test is built on the minimum wage of €920 a month, plus 50% for a second adult and 30% per child. For living, that figure is below the median rent on an ordinary flat in Lisbon, so the legal minimum and a workable budget are two different questions.

? Why does this page not give a monthly budget?
Because the household expenditure survey behind such budgets could not be read at source. The figure in circulation, €24,190 a year, comes from secondary retellings, and the statistical office's own portal returns nothing for the 2022/2023 wave.

? Is the rent figure based on asking prices?
No. It is computed from new leases actually signed and registered — 39,395 of them in the first quarter of 2026. That is what separates it from an index built on advertisements.
