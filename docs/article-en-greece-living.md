## Article

Every guide to living in Greece prints numbers. A one-bedroom in the centre for €400–700. A dozen eggs for €3.88. Utilities between €120 and €309. None of them says where those numbers came from, and the two pages that rank highest for this subject are both published by firms that sell golden visas.

This page is built the other way round. Each figure below is followed by the thing that establishes it: a survey, a ministerial decision, a circular, a dataset. Where the figure does not exist in any source we could read, the page says so instead of estimating.

### How to move to Greece legally: the four routes, briefly

Before any of the costs matter, there has to be a lawful basis for being here, and there are four.

A citizen of an EU member state needs none of this and can skip the section. Everyone else chooses between an investor permit, a permit for people living on their own income, and the digital nomad route.

The investor permit — the one the market calls the golden visa — asks for **€800,000** in Attica, the Thessaloniki regional unit, Mykonos, Thira and islands above 3,100 inhabitants, **€400,000** in the rest of the country, and **€250,000** in two narrow cases: conversion of premises to residential use, and restoration of a listed building. The full arithmetic, including which of those tiers carries a minimum floor area, is set out in our guide to the Greek golden visa.

The income route, formally the FIP visa, asks for no purchase at all — only **€3,500 a month** in demonstrable income, set by ministerial decision.

Two things about these routes matter for daily life rather than for the application, and neither is usually stated. The investor permit does not let you work in Greece. And the years spent holding either permit do not automatically count towards permanent residence: the statute counts days spent in the country, not the type of permit held.

### Cost of living in Greece by region: what the state's own survey says

The Hellenic Statistical Authority runs a Household Budget Survey. Its 2024 edition puts average household expenditure at **€1,724.54 a month**, or €20,694.48 a year, up 3.6% on 2023.

That number is the honest starting point, and it is also the number no competitor page carries, because it describes a Greek household rather than an imagined expat one. What it buys is a structure:

| Category | € per month | Share of budget |
|---|---|---|
| Food and non-alcoholic beverages | 356.68 | 20.7% |
| Housing | 247.51 | 14.4% |
| Transport | 229.75 | 13.3% |
| Restaurants, cafés and hotels | 203.87 | 11.8% |
| Health | 134.46 | 7.8% |

Households that rent rather than own spend **17.1% of total expenditure on rent alone**, which is the single most useful ratio on this page for anyone deciding whether a given rent is sustainable.

**The regional gap is larger than most people expect.** Attica averages **€2,030.27 a month** — 117.7% of the national figure — while Sterea Ellada averages **€1,184.58**, or 68.7%. The most expensive region costs almost twice what the cheapest does, and that spread is a state statistic rather than an impression.

One caveat the survey states about itself: the 2024 edition was rebuilt on the COICOP-HBS 2018 classification, so it cannot be compared directly with earlier years. Anyone telling you Greek living costs rose by a specific percentage since 2019 is comparing two different instruments.

### Rent in Greece: what is asked and what is signed

There is no state statistic for rent by city. That is worth saying plainly, because every page on this subject prints one anyway.

What exists is two commercial datasets that measure genuinely different things, and reading them side by side is more informative than either alone.

**RE/MAX Greece publishes averages from concluded leases** across 124 areas. Attica averages **€9.2 per square metre**; Kolonaki €14, Lycabettus €13, Petralona €10.4. Thessaloniki averages **€7.7**, with the old waterfront at €14 and the historic centre at €11.4. On the islands and Crete: Heraklion **€8.5**, Rethymno **€11**, Mykonos **€12**, Santorini **€14**. Residential rents nationwide rose **3.8%** over the year.

**Spitogatos Insights publishes asking prices from listings** for the second quarter of 2026. In Attica these run from **€7.5** in Agios Stefanos to **€22.0** in Vouliagmeni, up 4.5% year on year. In Thessaloniki, from **€4.9** in Chortiatis to **€12.0** in the centre and Vardaris–Lachanokipi, with Mygdonia up 25%. The Cyclades are the most expensive part of the country at **€14.4**, ahead of Lefkada at €11.8.

Put the two together and the useful fact appears: the Attica average from signed leases, €9.2, sits near the bottom of the €7.5–22.0 asking range, not in the middle of it. Listings describe what landlords hope for. Leases describe what tenants agreed to. A budget built on the first will be wrong in a predictable direction.

![3]

Both of these are commercial sources — an agency and a portal — and neither is a public register. Spitogatos does not publish its sample size. We use them because nothing better exists for rent at city level, and we say what they are rather than presenting them as official.

### Best place to live in Greece: what the numbers decide and what they do not

Two of the figures above answer this question honestly and the rest of the internet answers it with adjectives.

Cost sets the floor. Attica costs 117.7% of the national average and rents there average €9.2 per square metre against €7.7 in Thessaloniki — so the same 80 square metres runs roughly €736 a month in Athens and €616 in Thessaloniki on concluded-lease figures. Crete sits between them: Heraklion €8.5, Rethymno €11. The islands invert the picture entirely, with the Cyclades the most expensive part of the country and Santorini and Mykonos at €14 and €12, because a rental market shaped by a four-month tourist season is not a residential market.

**Cheapest place to live in Greece**, on regional expenditure, is Sterea Ellada at €1,184.58 a month — 31% below the national average. That is the answer to the question as asked. Whether it is the right answer depends on a variable no dataset holds: winter. An island or a small mainland town in August and the same place in February are different countries in terms of open restaurants, ferry schedules and available medical care, and no cost table captures that.

What the numbers cannot decide: which of these has the doctor, the school and the flight connection you need. That is a question for a visit in low season, not for a page.

### Living in Greece as a foreigner: AFM, AMKA and what your permit does not include

Three registrations turn a permit into a functioning life, and they are separate from the immigration file.

The **AFM** is the tax number, issued by the tax authority, and nothing works without it — not a lease, not a utility contract, not a bank account. The **AMKA** is the social security number and the key to the public health system. Registration with **e-EFKA** is what a self-employed person does to be lawfully in business.

That last one carries a real monthly cost, and it is where the golden-visa guides go quiet. For 2026, e-EFKA sets self-employed contributions by category: **€150.46** for the special category, then **€250.77**, **€300.93**, **€360.63**, **€433.47**, **€519.45** and **€675.87**, each with a further **€10** a month for unemployment insurance. The figures are set by circular 6/2026 and adjusted annually against the previous year's consumer price index.

The reason this matters to an investor rather than a freelancer: **the investment permit does not carry the right to work in Greece.** Someone who holds it and wants to earn here needs a different basis, and that basis has a monthly price which is not in any brochure.

For context on the local pay scale, the statutory minimum wage rose to **€920 a month** and **€41.09 a day** on 1 April 2026, by Ministerial Decision 8934/2026 published in Government Gazette 1759/Β/27-3-2026. It replaced €880. This is the number to hold against any salary offer, and against the rents above.

### Bad things about living in Greece: the pros and cons nobody selling a visa will write

Every page on this subject is published by someone who profits from your arrival, so here is the part they leave out.

**The administration is slow and the wait is not published as a rule.** As of November 2025 there were 13,499 undecided residence applications, 10,703 of them in Attica. The law sets no issuing deadline for this permit at all. What protects an applicant in the meantime is the certificate issued on filing, which grants lawful stay until a decision — but the decision itself arrives when it arrives.

**Costs vary by region far more than by lifestyle.** The near-double gap between Attica and Sterea Ellada means the choice of town matters more to a budget than most personal habits do.

**Rent is rising faster than the headline suggests in specific places.** Nationwide rents rose 3.8% on concluded leases, but Mygdonia in the Thessaloniki area rose 25% on asking prices in a year. Averages hide that.

**And the electricity figure does not exist publicly.** Eurostat gives an EU average of €0.2896 per kWh for the second half of 2025 and does not break out Greece in either of its published tables for that period; the only mention of Greece is that no support measures were taken in that semester. We could have taken a number from the incumbent supplier's price list and presented it as the cost of electricity in Greece. We have not, because a single company's tariff on a single date is not a national statistic.

### Best place to retire in Greece, and the 7% rate that makes it work

This is the one part of the subject where search interest is growing rather than shrinking.

Greece taxes qualifying foreign pensioners at a flat **7%** on foreign-source income, under article 5B of the income tax code, for fifteen tax years, subject to a prior non-residence test of five of the last six years. It sits alongside article 5A — the flat €100,000 regime — and article 5C, which grants a 50% exemption for seven years.

Combine that with the regional figures and the arithmetic is straightforward: a pension taxed at 7% goes considerably further in a region averaging €1,184.58 a month than in Attica at €2,030.27. The tax regime and the region are two separate decisions, and most people make only the first.

### Frequently asked questions

**How much do you need to live in Greece per month?**
The average Greek household spends €1,724.54 a month according to the 2024 Household Budget Survey — €2,030.27 in Attica, €1,184.58 in Sterea Ellada. A household renting rather than owning puts 17.1% of its total spending into rent.

**Is it expensive to live in Greece?**
Relative to the local minimum wage of €920 a month, yes. Relative to northern Europe, no. Both statements are true and the second one is the one guides print.

**What is the average rent in Greece?**
There is no official figure. Concluded leases average €9.2 per square metre in Attica and €7.7 in Thessaloniki; asking prices in listings run from €7.5 to €22.0 in Attica.

**Can I work in Greece on a golden visa?**
No. The investment permit does not carry the right to work. Self-employment requires registration with e-EFKA and monthly contributions from €150.46 upwards.

**Where is the cheapest place to live in Greece?**
By regional household expenditure, Sterea Ellada at €1,184.58 a month. The islands are the opposite: the Cyclades are the most expensive part of the country.

**Do I have to live in Greece to keep the permit?**
To hold the permit, no. To reach permanent residence, yes — the statute counts days spent in the country, not years of permit ownership.
