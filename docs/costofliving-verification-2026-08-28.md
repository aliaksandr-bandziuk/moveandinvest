# Cost of living: verification

Compiled 28 August 2026. Every figure below is either traced to the instrument
or dataset that states it, or marked as unverified. The article may use nothing
that is not on this page.

Cyprus is absent throughout. Its status on the site is `planned`, and the
section in `sourceData.ts` already records that its criteria could not be read
in a primary source. Adding it here would be adding a jurisdiction on secondary
material to a piece whose subject is the difference between the two.

## Part 1 — what the law makes an applicant prove

### The finding this part rests on

Three of the four investment routes impose **no income test at all**. The fourth,
Portugal, imposes the lowest income bar on the site. Meanwhile the non-investment
routes into the same four countries test income hard — up to €3,500 a month in
Greece. Buying in exempts you from proving you can afford to stay.

That claim is the piece, so each half of it is sourced separately below.

#### The golden visa does NOT waive the income test — verified

This is the sharpest claim in the piece and it is now on primary sources.

- **Lei 23/2007, art. 90.º-A(1)(a)**, current consolidated text (19th version,
  updated to Lei n.º 61/2025, de 22/10), read at PGD Lisboa, the
  Procuradoria-Geral Distrital de Lisboa legal database:
  *"Preencham os requisitos gerais estabelecidos no artigo 77.º, **com exceção
  da alínea a) do n.º 1**"*
  https://pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?artigo_id=920A0090A&ficha=1&nid=920&nversao=&pagina=1&so_miolo=&tabela=leis
- **Lei 23/2007, art. 77.º n.º 1**, read in the original Diário da República PDF
  (1.ª série n.º 127, 4 July 2007):
  *"a) Posse de visto de residência válido...; ... **d) Posse de meios de
  subsistência, tal como definidos pela portaria a que se refere a alínea d) do
  n.º 1 do artigo 52.º**; e) Alojamento; ..."*
  https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2007/07/12700/42904330.pdf

The exception in 90.º-A is singular and explicit: it disapplies **alínea a)**,
the residence-visa requirement, and nothing else. **Alínea d), means of
subsistence, survives.** So the ARI waives the visa, not the income proof.

Amendments checked: Lei 23/2007 was amended three times in 2025 — Lei 9/2025
(13/02), Lei 55-C/2025 (22/07) and Lei 61/2025 (22/10). The amendment lists in
art. 2.º of Lei 9/2025 and Lei 61/2025 name neither art. 77.º nor art. 90.º-A;
the consolidated text incorporating Lei 55-C/2025 still shows art. 90.º-A in the
form quoted.

- Lei 61/2025 art. 2.º: https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2025/10/20400/0000900017.pdf
- Lei 9/2025 art. 2.º: https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2025/02/03100/0000600014.pdf

**Two residual caveats, both stated in the article or dropped from it:**

1. The alínea list in art. 77.º is quoted from the 2007 original, not from the
   current consolidation — PGD Lisboa's article page for 77.º errors and the DRE
   consolidated view is JavaScript-gated. Alínea c) has since gained a
   cross-reference and h)–j) appear to have been dropped, but **d) is stable
   across all nineteen versions**, which is what 90.º-A's cross-reference needs.
2. **Portaria 1563/2007 is presumed still in force, not proven.** No repealing
   or replacing instrument was found. Positive evidence: Decreto Regulamentar
   n.º 1/2024, de 17 de janeiro — the seventh amendment to DR 84/2007 — refers
   to Portaria 1563/2007 in its operative text, and a January 2024 regulation
   would not cross-refer to a repealed portaria. The DRE status line could not
   be read. Confidence: medium-high, not certainty.

#### D8 digital nomad — no statutory threshold

**Lei 23/2007, art. 61.º-B contains no income figure and no multiple of the
minimum wage.** The widely circulated "four times the minimum wage" (€3,680 a
month at the 2026 RMMG) could not be traced to any portaria, decreto or
despacho; it appears only on consultancy and law-firm pages. AIMA's own
checklists were unreachable, so an administratively fixed figure cannot be ruled
out. **The article must not state the 4× figure as law.**

**Two corrections to what the market publishes, both worth stating in the
article:**

1. **There is no MPRP income requirement in euros.** The regulations carry only
   a qualitative requirement of stable and regular resources sufficient to live
   without recourse to social assistance. Any euro "MPRP income requirement" is
   a figure that does not exist in the instrument.
2. **The Gozo and south-of-Malta discount is gone.** L.N. 146 of 2025 replaced
   the two-tier property and rent floors with a single national one. The
   original L.N. 121 of 2021 read: *"(a) ... three hundred and fifty thousand
   euro (€350,000) for a property situated in Malta; or (b) three hundred
   thousand euro (€300,000) for a property situated in the South of Malta or
   Gozo"*, and for rent €12,000 / €10,000.
   https://residencymalta.gov.mt/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/L.N.-121-of-2021.pdf
   Most consultancy pages still publish the superseded figures.

Fees under the current First Schedule, for context: administrative fee €60,000;
government contribution €37,000, now the same whether the applicant buys or
rents; €7,500 per adult dependant; €2,000 NGO donation.

**Caveat on the nomad permit.** €42,000 is stated on two official Residency
Malta pages (https://nomad.residencymalta.gov.mt/nomad-eligibility/ and its
FAQ), but **no subsidiary legislation sets it**. The permit's only S.L. —
S.L. 123.210, Nomad Residence Permits (Income Tax) Rules — governs the 10% tax
treatment, not eligibility. The article should say the threshold is
administrative rather than legislative, because that is a real difference in how
easily it changes.

**Caveat on the minimum wage.** The figures are from DIER, the responsible
department. The legal-notice number for 2026 (L.N. 289 of 2025) comes from
Eurofound, an EU agency, not from a gazette text. DIER's own consolidated
S.L. 452.71 PDF is stale — its schedule stops at the 2021 rate — and must not be
cited for a current figure.

## Part 2 — what living actually costs

### The methodological finding, which is half the article

**The national household budget surveys are not comparable with each other.**
Greece's latest wave is 2024, Portugal's is 2022/2023, Malta's is 2015, and the
UAE has published nothing since 2014. Putting those four numbers in one table
would produce a comparison of four different years and four methodologies while
looking like a comparison of four countries. The article states this instead of
doing it.

**The one instrument that is comparable is Eurostat's price level index**, which
is computed by one office, on one method, for the same year — and it covers only
the three EU members. The UAE cannot be placed on that scale at all.

**Provenance caveat, and it applies to both 2025 values.** Eurostat's databrowser
and its Statistics Explained tables are JavaScript-rendered and returned no
country rows to a fetcher; the dissemination API was unreachable. The Portugal
86.6 and Malta 91.9 values were read from **Statistics Norway's rendering of the
same Eurostat series** — an official statistical office, but one step removed:
https://www.ssb.no/en/priser-og-prisindekser/konsumpriser/statistikk/sammenlikning-av-prisniva-i-europa

Eurostat's own prose corroborates the magnitude, placing both countries among
those *"at less than 20% below the EU average"*. Eurostat flags 2025 as
preliminary and *"subject to revision following the calculation to be completed
in December 2026"* — the article says preliminary.

**Before publication, one manual lookup in `prc_ppp_ind_1` would put all three
2025 values on Eurostat's own table and remove this caveat.** It is a two-minute
job on an unrestricted connection.
