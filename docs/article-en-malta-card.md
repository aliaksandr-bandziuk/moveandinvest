## Article

There is no such thing as a Malta TRC card. The phrase is imported — from Portugal, where the residence title really is abbreviated that way — and Maltese sources do not use it anywhere. What Malta issues is the **eResidence card**, and the agency that issues it is **Identità**.

That is not pedantry. It is the reason so little accurate information exists about this document: people search for a name the issuing authority never uses, and land on pages written by whoever guessed the name too. Everything below comes from Identità's own published answers.

### Malta residence permit processing time: eight to ten weeks

Applications by non-EU nationals go through the Expatriates Portal, and the agency states the timeframe plainly:

> "An application may take between 8 -10 weeks to process from the date of submission. This time frame may vary since every application is processed on a case-by-case basis."

For EU and EEA nationals the mechanism is different and much faster at the front end: the eResidence document there is issued under a constitutive system, and Identità undertakes to respond to a request within **48 working hours** with a date and time for the biometrics appointment. That document is generally issued for **five years**, and for dependants its validity mirrors the sponsor's.

### The interim receipt, and why a first application grounds you

![1]

This is the single most consequential thing on this page, and it is published in one line that almost nobody quotes.

While the application is being processed you hold a blue or white **interim receipt**. Asked directly whether it can be used to travel, Identità answers:

> "If your residence permit application is still being processed, you should wait until the residence card is issued before travelling. The blue/white interim receipt is not a travel document, and this is also indicated on the receipt itself. For this reason, it cannot be used for travel or re-entry into Malta."

Read together with the eight-to-ten-week processing time, that means a first application is a commitment to stay on the island for roughly two to three months. Not a recommendation — a document that says on its face it is not a travel document.

This is where Malta differs sharply from Greece, and the contrast is worth knowing before you choose between them. In Greece the certificate issued on filing confers lawful residence and the rights of the permit until a decision comes, so a long Greek wait costs less than it looks. A long Maltese wait costs you the ability to leave and return.

The position on **renewals** is different and much easier:

> "If the applicant holds a valid passport and a valid residence card (in the case of renewal applications), travel should generally be possible."

The agency adds that re-entry should be confirmed with Immigration or the airport authorities, since that is not its own decision to make.

### Renewing a Malta residence card: the window is narrower than it looks

Most people assume a renewal can be filed whenever it is convenient. It cannot. Identità sets both ends:

> "a renewal application should be submitted no later than 6 weeks before the expiration date of the applicant's residence permit and no earlier than 3 months prior to its expiration date."

So the window is about six weeks wide, opening three months out and closing six weeks out. File too early and it is not accepted; file too late and you are into the gap that the interim receipt does not cover.

Holders of a permit issued through **Residency Malta** — the permanent residence programme, the older residence and visa programme, or the nomad permit — renew through that agency rather than through Identità. Two different agencies, two different portals, and the one you use depends on the instrument you hold rather than on the card in your pocket.

### Collecting the card

Approval does not end the process; the card has to be fetched, and only by one person.

> "Once the application is fully approved, the Third Country National will receive a collection notification with a PIN at the address provided to Identità."

Collection is at Identità in **Msida, Monday to Friday between 07:30 and 13:00**, with **no appointment** needed. You bring the collection letter, the interim receipt and your passport. And:

> "Residence permits must be collected personally by the applicant. Collection by a third party is not permitted."

Two practical consequences follow. The address on file has to be one where post reaches you, because the PIN goes there. And you have to be in Malta, in the morning, on a working day — which is the second time the process assumes you are physically present.

### What a Malta residence permit costs, by basis

![2]

The fee is not a single number. It depends entirely on the basis you applied under, and the spread is wide:

| Fee | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| €500 | Long-term residence |
| €100 | Economic self-sufficiency; posted workers; renewals under the permanent residence scheme |
| €50 | Study; post-study; family reunification under S.L. 217.06; family member policy; partner, child or parent of a Maltese citizen; traineeship; medical reasons; religious reasons; temporary grounds |
| Free | Exempt persons; victims of human trafficking; volunteers |

One detail inside the economic self-sufficiency line is worth pulling out, because it is an option rather than a charge: the first application costs €100, and **at renewal an applicant may choose to pay a further €100 to receive a two-year permit** instead of a one-year one. Paying twice to halve the number of renewals is, for anyone counting the cost of the process rather than the fee, usually the cheaper decision.

### The portal rules that quietly end applications

These are administrative details until one of them ends your file, at which point they are the only thing that matters. All four are published.

**A document request has a sixty-day fuse.** If Identità asks for more documents and nothing is resubmitted, "the system automatically withdraws such applications after 60 days if no resubmission is made by the applicant." No refusal, no decision — a withdrawal, performed by software.

**Uploads are capped at 2 MB per file.** A scanned passport at full resolution exceeds that easily, and the portal simply refuses it.

**Ten applications per account per thirty days.** The portal enforces the cap and, in the agency's words, the restriction "cannot be overridden". Families filing for several members at once run into it.

**A travel document under review locks the account.** While Identità is reviewing the passport attached to a first application, no further applications can be submitted from that account at all.

### If your application is refused

Refusals go to the **Immigration Appeals Board**, and Identità directs appeal questions to it rather than answering them itself. That is the venue; the agency that refused you is not the one that reviews the refusal.

What the published material does not settle is the deadline for appealing, or what a successful appeal restores. Those are questions for a Maltese lawyer with the refusal letter in hand, and any page that gives you a confident number for them is guessing.

### Frequently asked questions

**What is a Malta TRC card?**
Nothing — Malta does not use the term. The document is the eResidence card, issued by Identità. "TRC" is borrowed from the Portuguese título de residência and does not appear in Maltese sources.

**How long does a Malta residence permit take?**
Eight to ten weeks from submission for non-EU nationals, processed case by case. For EU and EEA nationals, Identità responds within 48 working hours to arrange biometrics.

**Can I travel while my Malta residence application is being processed?**
On a first application, no. The blue or white interim receipt is expressly not a travel document and cannot be used for travel or re-entry into Malta. On a renewal, travel is generally possible on a valid passport and the existing valid card.

**When can I renew my Malta residence card?**
No earlier than three months before it expires and no later than six weeks before. Holders of Residency Malta instruments — the permanent residence programme or the nomad permit — renew through that agency instead.

**How much does a Malta residence permit cost?**
€500 for long-term residence, €100 for economic self-sufficiency and posted workers, €50 for most other bases including study and family routes, and nothing for exempt persons, trafficking victims and volunteers.

**Where do I collect my Malta residence card?**
At Identità in Msida, Monday to Friday between 07:30 and 13:00, without an appointment, bringing the collection letter, the interim receipt and your passport. A notification with a PIN is posted to the address on file, and only the applicant may collect it.

**How long is a Malta residence card valid?**
For EU and EEA nationals the eResidence document is generally issued for five years, and a dependant's validity matches the sponsor's. For non-EU nationals it follows the basis of the permit, and under economic self-sufficiency a two-year permit can be obtained at renewal for an extra €100.

**What happens if I miss a document request?**
The portal withdraws the application automatically after sixty days.
