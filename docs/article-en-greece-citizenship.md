## Article

Every page in this market gives the same answer to how long Greek naturalisation takes. Seven years.

That answer is correct for one group of people and wrong by five years for another, and the pages giving it do not say which group you are in — because they are not reading the article that decides it.

The Greek Citizenship Code, Law 3284/2004, sets **three** different periods. Which one applies to you turns on your nationality and on a closed list of residence titles that most pages never mention.

### The three periods

![1]

**Three continuous years.** Article 5(1)(δ) gives the short period to exactly four categories, and the enumeration is worth reading closely because one of them carries a condition that is easy to miss:

- nationals of an **EU member state**;
- a **spouse of a Greek citizen with a child** — «τους συζύγους Έλληνα ή Ελληνίδας **με τέκνο**». A spouse without a child does not qualify for the short period;
- anyone with **parental custody of a Greek-national child born in Greece**;
- **stateless persons**.

The provision asks for «μια τουλάχιστον συνεχή τριετία» — at least one continuous three-year period.

**Seven continuous years.** The first sentence of the same subparagraph: «Να διαμένει στην Ελλάδα νόμιμα για **επτά συνεχή έτη** πριν από την υποβολή της αίτησης». Legal residence in Greece for seven continuous years before the application is submitted. This is the figure the market publishes, and it is right for everybody outside the four categories above **who holds one of the residence titles on the list described next**.

**Twelve continuous years.** Article 5(3): «διαμένει στην Ελλάδα νόμιμα για **δώδεκα (12) συνεχή έτη** πριν από την υποβολή της αίτησης». A holder of any other valid residence title, temporary ones excepted, naturalises at twelve rather than seven.

**And no period at all** for ομογενείς — people of Greek descent — and for those born in Greece who have lived there continuously since.

### The list that decides between seven and twelve

Article 5(1)(ε) requires the applicant to hold one of an **exhaustive list** of residence titles, lettered αα) to αι). It is not a formality. It is the switch between the seven-year route and the twelve-year one.

On that list, at item **αθ)**, sits «άδεια διαμονής για επενδυτική δραστηριότητα» — the residence permit for investment activity, which is the Greek golden visa. Property-owner permits are named there too.

So for a reader who came to this subject through the investment route, seven years is the right number, and it is right because of a provision almost no page selling that route cites.

For a reader on some other title — and there are many kinds of Greek residence permit — the honest answer is that the number depends on whether that specific title is on the list, and if it is not, article 5(3) asks for twelve.

**One caution we are printing because we got it wrong ourselves.** Until 8 September 2026 our own Greece guide criticised a law firm by name for publishing "12 years continuous residence required for Greek citizenship", and gave the correction as "Seven". Twelve years is a real provision of the Code. It is the wrong figure for a golden visa holder and the right one for somebody else, and we had read only the seven-year sentence before calling it wrong. That correction has been withdrawn.

### The conditions are in two articles, and the examination is in the second one

This is the other thing the market gets structurally wrong: it treats naturalisation as one article. The Code splits it.

![2]

**Article 5 — formal conditions (τυπικές προϋποθέσεις).** Legal age at the time the declaration is submitted; no final conviction for an intentional offence in the last decade, with a suspended sentence still counting; no pending expulsion or unresolved question about the lawfulness of the applicant's stay; the residence period; and the qualifying title.

**Article 5Α — substantive conditions (ουσιαστικές προϋποθέσεις).** Sufficient knowledge of the Greek language «ώστε να εκπληρώνει τις υποχρεώσεις που απορρέουν από την» — so as to discharge the obligations arising from citizenship. Sufficient knowledge of Greek history and geography, of Greek culture and customs. Normal integration into the economic and social life of the country, for which the article names the indicators weighed, beginning with stable employment. A fourth limb, (γ), has been repealed.

**Article 5Β** holds the security grounds, and articles 6 to 9 the documents, the procedure, the decision and the oath.

**No CEFR level appears in article 5Α.** The B1 in general circulation, and the 70% pass mark for the ΠΕΓΠ examination, come from the level below the statute. That is not a reason to disbelieve them; it is a reason to know which of the two your plan depends on, because an administrative standard changes without a parliament. The same gap exists in Portuguese law, where the market prints A2 for a statute that names nothing.

### What the periods are counted from, and the word that does the work

«Συνεχή» — continuous. In all three tiers.

Greek law offers **no window inside which broken periods may be summed**. This is where it differs sharply from Portugal, whose article 15(3) lets interrupted residence add up so long as it fits inside a six, nine or twelve year frame. In Greece a gap does not shorten the total. It starts it again.

That has a practical consequence for anyone holding a residence permit that requires no physical presence. **The Code counts residence, and the investor permit imposes no minimum stay** — so holding the permit for seven years and living elsewhere does not produce seven years of anything. The permit is a qualifying title; it is not a substitute for being there.

You can put your own dates into our [naturalisation clock](/naturalisation-clock), which now carries all three Greek tiers alongside Portugal and Malta.

### The historic figure, and why a stale page will quote it

If a page tells you ten years within the last twelve, it is quoting the **original 2004 text**, which required «δέκα συνολικά έτη την τελευταία δωδεκαετία» — ten years in total within the last twelve — with five for stateless persons and recognised refugees and five for Olympic athletes. That text is superseded. Its survival on advice pages is a useful test: a page still printing it has not opened the Code since before the reform that replaced it.

### Where this text was read, and how current it is

The Ministry of the Interior publishes a consolidated text of the Citizenship Code at ypes.gr, **consolidated to 17 September 2025**, prepared under licence by a legal-information provider. The ministry's own header says the edition is informational and subject to checking against the Government Gazette, and we repeat that here rather than burying it: this page rests on the ministry's consolidation, not on the Gazette itself.

**One thing we have not resolved, and will not assert.** Item αθ) of the qualifying list cites the investment permit "under article 16 of Law 4251/2014" — the old Migration Code, which Law 5038/2023 replaced, and where the investor permit is now article 100. The consolidation is dated well after that replacement and still carries the old citation. Greek codifying statutes commonly provide that references to a repealed law are read as references to the corresponding provisions of the new one; we have not found or excluded such a provision in Law 5038/2023, whose final articles we could not reach. So we are naming the question and not answering it.

### Questions

? How many years of residence does Greek citizenship require?
Three, seven or twelve, depending on who you are and what permit you hold. Three continuous years for nationals of an EU member state, for a spouse of a Greek with a child, for someone with parental custody of a Greek-national child born in Greece, and for stateless persons. Seven for everybody else holding a residence title on the closed list of article 5(1)(ε), which includes the investor permit. Twelve under article 5(3) for a holder of any other valid non-temporary title.

? Does the Greek golden visa count towards citizenship?
The permit is a qualifying title — it is named at item αθ) of article 5(1)(ε), so its holder is on the seven-year period rather than twelve. But the Code counts residence, and the investor permit imposes no minimum stay. Holding it for seven years while living elsewhere accumulates nothing.

? Is there Greek citizenship by investment?
No. Greek law contains no provision granting citizenship in exchange for an investment. The investment route grants a residence permit, and that permit puts its holder on the ordinary naturalisation period like any other listed title.

? What language level is required for Greek citizenship?
The statute names none. Article 5Α asks for sufficient knowledge of the language, and of Greek history, geography, culture and customs. The B1 level and the 70% pass mark widely quoted for the ΠΕΓΠ examination come from below the statute, and an administrative standard can be changed without a new law.

? Can the seven years be interrupted?
No. All three periods are «συνεχή» — continuous — and the Code provides no window inside which broken periods may be added together, unlike Portugal. A gap restarts the count.

? Why do some pages say ten or twelve years?
Twelve is real: it is article 5(3), for a title outside the qualifying list. Ten within twelve is the original 2004 wording, since superseded, and a page still printing it has not reopened the Code in years.
