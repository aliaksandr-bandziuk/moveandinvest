# Portugal for the British — verification, 8 September 2026

For `moving-to-portugal-from-the-uk` (plan item D2). Everything the article may
assert about the UK-specific layer sits here **verbatim**. Two claims were
drafted and killed during this check; both are recorded at the bottom, because
the near-miss is more useful to a future reader than the finding.

---

## 1. The convention was replaced

Read 8 September 2026 at HMRC's Double Taxation Relief Manual, DT15600
(Portugal: agreements in force):
https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt15600

**Current convention**
- Signed: **15 September 2025**
- Entered into force: **29 December 2025**
- Effect in Portugal: from **1 January 2026**, "in respect of all taxes covered"
- Effect in the UK: **1 January 2026** for withheld taxes, **1 April 2026** for
  corporation tax, **6 April 2026** for income tax and capital gains tax

**Previous convention**
- Signed: **27 March 1968**
- Entered into force: **17 January 1969**
- DT15600: "This previous convention applied until the dates from which the
  above convention took effect."

**Fifty-seven years between the two signatures**, and the arithmetic is the
plain one: March 1968 to September 2025.

**THE EFFECT DATES DO NOT MATCH, and that is the structural fact.** Portugal
began applying the new convention on 1 January 2026; the United Kingdom did not
apply it to income tax or capital gains until 6 April 2026. For those months the
two states were applying different instruments to the same person. This is
ordinary treaty practice — each state's dates follow its own tax year — and it
is not a defect. It is simply absent from every page in the results.

## 2. Pensions, both conventions, verbatim

**2025 convention, Article 17 (Pensions):**

> "Subject to the provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 18, pensions and other
> similar remuneration paid to a resident of a Contracting State, shall be
> taxable only in that State."

**2025 convention, Article 18(1) (Government Service):**

> "Salaries, wages, pensions, and other similar remuneration paid by (or out of
> funds created by) a Contracting State ("the paying State") or a political or
> administrative subdivision or a local authority thereof to an individual in
> respect of services rendered to the paying State or subdivision or authority
> thereof shall be taxable only in the paying State. However, where that
> individual is a not a national of the paying State but is a national of the
> other Contracting State, such salaries, wages, pensions, and other similar
> remuneration may be taxed in both Contracting States."

**1968 convention, Article 17(1):**

> "Any pensions and other similar remuneration (other than pensions or
> remuneration to which Article 18 applies) paid in consideration of past
> employment to a resident of a Contracting State and any annuity paid to such a
> resident shall be taxable only in that State."

**BOTH SAY THE STATE OF RESIDENCE.** The wording was modernised and the
carve-out for government service moved from a parenthesis into a cross-
reference; the rule did not move. A private pension paid to a resident of
Portugal is taxable only in Portugal, and was already.

Article 28 governs entry into force: the convention enters into force on the
later of the two written notifications through diplomatic channels, and takes
effect on the dates recorded in section 1.

## 3. Two claims drafted and killed

Recorded because both are the same failure that produced the D8 threshold error
on 7 September: a summary read in place of the instrument.

**KILLED CLAIM 1 — "HMRC's own summary is out of date."** DT15602, the Portugal
treaty summary, was updated **4 August 2026** — four months after the new
convention took effect in the UK — and its row for non-government pensions reads
"Taxable only in the UK". Read against Article 17, that looks like the old
source-state rule left standing on gov.uk.

It is not. The Double Taxation Relief Manual describes relief **for a UK
resident on income arising in the other state**. From that side, "taxable only
in the UK" is Article 17 stated correctly: the recipient is UK-resident, so the
residence state is the UK. The page names no convention version, which is what
made the misreading available. **Do not write that HMRC prints the wrong rule.**

**KILLED CLAIM 2 — "the new convention reversed pension taxation."** This was
the intended spine of the article: a new treaty moving a British retiree's
pension from the UK to Portugal, on the single question that audience cares
about most. Article 17(1) of the 1968 convention says the same thing as Article
17 of the 2025 one. **There was no reversal**, and publishing one would have
been a loud falsehood on the most sensitive point on the page. The 1968 text was
opened only because the claim was about to be written.

## 4. What was checked in the market

**Blevins Franks, "Moving to Portugal from UK – residency and taxation", dated
11 February 2025, opened in full on 8 September 2026 (9 574 characters).** It is
the only page on the first screen written by tax advisers.

- No occurrence of "treaty", "convention", "double tax", "1968" or "2026".
- The convention is absent in both its old and its new form.
- It does carry NHR correctly as closed to new applicants, with existing holders
  keeping their ten-year term.

The date explains it: the page predates the signature of the new convention by
seven months. This is stated as a fact about one page opened in full, not as a
claim about the market.

**The rest of the first screen, from the results page itself:** GOV.UK's own
"Living in Portugal" guide, two removals companies (Anglo Pacific, White &
Company), Wise, a UK immigration firm, imin-portugal, Reddit twice and a
Facebook group. **Their contents were not opened**, so nothing may be asserted
about them beyond what their own titles and dates say.

## 5. Out of scope, stated so a later reader knows it was a decision

- **The UK Statutory Residence Test.** UK law, published by HMRC. Named, never
  recited with day counts — the same rule that keeps US filing thresholds off
  the American page.
- **Rates on dividends, interest and royalties.** The current summary gives 10%,
  10/5/0 and 5%; the 1968 rates were not read, so **no claim of change or
  continuity may be made about them**.
- **IFICI.** Not read for this article.
- **Naturalisation.** A British citizen is on the same ten years as an American —
  neither EU nor CPLP. It belongs to `portugal-citizenship` and to the D1 page;
  this one links and does not repeat.
- **`moving to portugal from ireland`** (170 collapsed, in the same export file):
  an Irish citizen is an EU citizen and his position is not the one described
  here. Must not be folded into this page.
