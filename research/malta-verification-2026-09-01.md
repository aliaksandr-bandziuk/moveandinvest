# Malta: what was checked, where it was read, and what could not be established

**Checked:** 1 September 2026
**Scope:** the Maltese relocation guide — the permanent residence programme, what a beneficiary actually owes after the certificate is issued, tax residence as distinct from residence, what replaced the citizenship scheme the Court of Justice struck down, and the two cheaper routes nobody compares it against.
**Companion documents:** `greece-verification-2026-08-28.md`, `uae-verification-2026-08-30.md`, `portugal-verification-2026-08-28.md`, `costofliving-verification-2026-08-28.md`.

## 0. How to read the tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | The operative legal text, read directly. |
| B | A government or authority page explaining its own rules. |
| C | A law firm, Big Four publication, trade press or news outlet. |
| D | Could not be established. |
| E | Asserted only by parties who sell the thing. Not usable. |

**What is different about Malta, and it cuts both ways.** Every instrument named below is on the government's own legislation portal, which is better than Greece. But `legislation.mt` serves its texts through a JavaScript PDF viewer, and the two copies of S.L. 217.26 that Residency Malta publishes as plain PDFs have no machine-readable text layer. So for the two instruments that matter most, this document rests on **A-metadata** — the portal's own record of a title, an enabling act, a date of first entry into force and a consolidation date — plus **tier B** for the substance. Where a finding needed the operative words and only the operative words would do, it is in §7 rather than stated.

**A consequence worth naming.** Nothing below should be read as "the regulations do not say X". The regulations may well say X. What this document can say is that the agency responsible does not publish X anywhere a reader or an agent would look, and that is a different and smaller claim.

## 1. Corrections to this project's own published statements

**1.1 The citizenship row on `/sources` tells the wrong story about how the scheme ended, and the true one is better.**

It says the ESDI scheme "was replaced by Act XXI of 2025 and the S.L. 188.06 rules".

S.L. 188.06 is not a new instrument. The legislation portal records it as first entering into force on **20 November 2020** — under the old name, as the regulations that ran the very scheme the Court struck down — with its current consolidated text published **29 July 2025**. Aġenzija Komunità Malta states plainly what happened in between:

> "Subsidiary legislation 188.06 was amended in order to provide for processes and requirements to regulate the granting of citizenship by naturalisation on the basis of merit."

Amended, by **L.N. 159 of 2025** — an instrument this project has never named. **Tier B** (Komunità Malta, 2 September 2025), **A-metadata** for the two dates.

So the sentence "the scheme was replaced" is not what the record shows. The scheme's own regulations were kept, renamed and rewritten. That is a materially different fact and, for a site that exists to trace what actually happened to a rule, the better one.

**1.2 The merit categories are listed incompletely, and the two that are missing are the two our reader would look for.**

`/sources` has "naturalisation for merit in science, sport, culture or philanthropy". The current official statement of the route lists:

> "exceptional contribution and services or exceptional interest in fields of science and research, technology, sport, entrepreneurship (including job creation), or culture and the arts, as well as philanthropy initiatives"

**Technology** and **entrepreneurship, including job creation**, are absent from our version. A reader of this site is, more often than not, someone with a business. **Tier B**, verbatim, from a document Komunità Malta uploaded in **February 2026** — the most recent official statement of the route that exists.

**1.3 Three things the row omits entirely**, each of which a reader would want before anything else:

- the merit route has **no published price**. The official document says only that "Fees that would be established by the Agency apply as regards the processing of the different stages of the application." For a site whose product is naming the number, the absence of a number is the finding, not a gap in the research;
- the formal application must demonstrate **residence, property, the exceptional service itself, and knowledge of the language** — four conditions, of which we published one;
- there is a separate **Office of the Regulator** for this route, under **articles 25, 25A and 25B of Cap. 188**.

**1.4 A correction to my own first reading, recorded because it nearly reached the page.** I took the legislation portal's ELI reference for the merit regulations to be `188.06`, matching how Komunità Malta writes it, and fetched nothing. The portal's slug is `188.6`. Same instrument, and the government's own two arms spell their own citation two ways — which is worth knowing before quoting either.

## 2. The instruments

**Malta Permanent Residence Programme Regulations, S.L. 217.26**, made under the Immigration Act (Cap. 217). First entry into force **29 March 2021**; consolidated text published **22 July 2025**. Amended by **L.N. 310 of 2024** (in force 1 January 2025) and **L.N. 146 of 2025** (22 July 2025). **A-metadata**, corroborated **tier B**: Residency Malta's own "Legal Framework" page lists S.L. 217.26 as amended by those two notices **and nothing later**, and the consolidation date matches L.N. 146 exactly.

This settles the question the guide has to answer first: **every MPRP figure already published on `/sources` is current as of today.** €375,000 purchase or €14,000 annual rent, €37,000 contribution, €60,000 administrative fee, €2,000 NGO donation, €7,500 per dependant, five-year holding period, and the asset test. Checked, unchanged, 1 September 2026.

**Maltese Citizenship Act, Cap. 188**, as amended by **Act XXI of 2025**, published **24 July 2025** in Government Gazette No. 21,474. **A-metadata.**

**Granting of Citizenship by Naturalisation on the basis of Merit Regulations, S.L. 188.06** (portal slug `188.6`), first in force 20 November 2020, current text published 29 July 2025, amended by **L.N. 159 of 2025**. **A-metadata** and **tier B**.

**Nomad Residence Permits (Income Tax) Rules, S.L. 123.210**, effective from 1 January 2024. **Tier B**, from the tax administration's own guidelines dated 12 March 2026.

**The Global Residence Programme Rules, 2013.** Published guidelines are **Version 2.0 of 2020**, and that is the most recent version the tax administration publishes — see §6 for why that matters.

**Status of Long-Term Residents (Third Country Nationals) Regulations, S.L. 217.05**, for the integration measures. **Tier B.**

## 3. The programme: what is published, and what is not

**Confirmed, and new to this project.** The agents' handbook imposes an ongoing obligation that no brochure mentions. At **§2.7, annual compliance**, a beneficiary must produce

> "proof of property lease, via the presentation of a contract of lease, as well as proof of sickness insurance cover"

every year for the first five years, and thereafter "whenever the Agency deems it necessary". **Tier B**, handbook v2.1 of 29 January 2024. The project already cites v4.0 of 28 January 2025 for the processing-time finding; the section numbering should be re-checked against v4.0 before the guide quotes it.

**Not published: any minimum stay.** This is the question every reader has and the one no Maltese source answers. Five places were walked on 1 September 2026:

1. **S.L. 217.26 on the legislation portal** — served through a viewer; no text retrievable.
2. **S.L. 217.26 as Residency Malta publishes it** (the February 2025 consolidated PDF) — no machine-readable text layer.
3. **The agents' handbook v2.1** — searched; the subject does not appear. The handbook itself says at §1.3 that "Should there be conflicting views between this Handbook and the legislation, it is the legislation that prevails", which is an agency telling you the answer is somewhere you cannot read.
4. **The MPRP frequently-asked-questions PDF** — the document that would answer it. The agency's own link now returns **404**; the file was withdrawn.
5. **Residency Malta's "Compliance" page** — renders "Nothing Found".

**Tier D.** The guide must therefore say that Malta does not publish a minimum-presence rule for this programme, and must not say that no such rule exists.

**What is published, and it points the other way.** Identità's rights-and-obligations guidance sets an absence rule for **long-term resident status**, not for this programme, and a separate rule for family-reunification permits whose holder is "spending the majority of their time residing in another country". Neither is addressed to an MPRP beneficiary. Schengen travel is the ordinary permit-holder rule: "provided that the total period of the visit/s does not exceed 90 days within a 180-day period". **Tier B.**

## 4. Tax, which is a different question from residence and is decided by different rules

Both quotations are verbatim from the Malta Tax and Customs Administration. **Tier B.**

> "When an individual is present in Malta for more than 183 days (in any particular year) they will be considered as tax residence in Malta for that year."

> "Those who are considered as ordinary resident and domiciled in Malta are subject to tax on a worldwide basis, whilst those who are either not domiciled or not ordinarily resident in Malta are only taxable on a remittance basis."

And "A person who lives in Malta on a permanent or indefinite basis is ordinarily resident in Malta."

**Three separate tests, and the guide's job is to keep them apart.** Whether you may live in Malta is decided by S.L. 217.26. Whether Malta taxes you is decided by a day count. What Malta taxes you on is decided by domicile and ordinary residence. The project's existing `/sources` row already says the MPRP "does not by itself confer tax residence"; these two sentences are why, and they are worth quoting rather than paraphrasing.

The remittance-basis detail already on `/sources` — foreign income taxed only when remitted, foreign capital gains untaxed even when remitted, €5,000 minimum tax where unremitted foreign income is €35,000 or more — was not re-read this session and is carried over from the check of 28 August 2026.

## 5. Citizenship after the judgment

**Ordinary naturalisation**, verbatim from Aġenzija Komunità Malta, under **articles 10(1) and 11 of Cap. 188**. **Tier B.**

> "resided in Malta throughout the twelve (12) months immediately preceding the date of application"

> "resided in Malta for an aggregate period of a minimum of four (4) years, during the six (6) years preceding the above period"

plus "adequate knowledge of Maltese or English language".

**The arithmetic is not five years and the guide should say so.** It is twelve continuous months, and four years inside the six that precede those twelve — a minimum of five years of residence which may be spread across as much as seven. Our own comparison row calls Malta "about five years", which is right as a floor and wrong as a picture.

**And the trap is the Greek one exactly.** Naturalisation counts *residence*. The programme that sells Maltese residence publishes no minimum presence (§3). The page does not exclude any permit — Komunità compiles a residence certificate for the applicant — so MPRP years are not disqualified; they simply have to have been years someone actually spent in Malta. Anyone who bought the certificate in order to be somewhere else has accumulated nothing. This is the same finding as `greece-residency`'s presence-versus-permit section and should link to it.

**The merit route**, under **article 10(9) of Cap. 188** and S.L. 188.06. **Tier B**, from the February 2026 Komunità document:

> "at least eight months of residence in Malta"

Categories as quoted in §1.2. No published fee. The process runs proposal letter → independent evaluation board with due diligence → recommendation to the Agency → Minister's approval in principle → formal application demonstrating residence, property, exceptional service and language → Evaluation Board → Minister's final decision → oath.

The Court of Justice ruling in **C-181/23 of 29 April 2025** is carried over from the check of 28 August 2026 and was not re-read this session.

## 6. The two routes the comparison never mentions, and one of them inverts the whole picture

**Nomad Residence Permit.** **Tier B**, from Residency Malta's nomad FAQ, **version 14.1, last updated 17 April 2026** — the most current agency document found for any Maltese route.

- minimum gross yearly income **€42,000**; applications submitted before 1 April 2024 keep the old **€32,400**;
- employed by a foreign employer, or self-employed through a foreign company, or freelancing for clients established abroad;
- valid **one year**, renewable three times, **maximum four years**;
- renewal requires "a bank statement showing payment transactions carried out in Malta as proof that they have resided in Malta for a cumulative period of at least five (5) months over the previous twelve (12) months";
- and, explicitly: "The fact that an applicant is issued with a Nomad Residence Permit does not necessarily mean that the applicant is entitled to be subject to Maltese tax."

Tax treatment under **S.L. 123.210**: **10% on income derived from authorised work**, with no tax chargeable "before the end of 12 months from the later of: a. the date on which the nomad residence permit is issued; or b. the 1st of January 2024". **Tier B**, guidelines of 12 March 2026.

**This is the finding the English guide should lead on.** The cheap route demands five months a year in Malta and proves it with bank statements. The route costing about €126,000 above the price of a flat demands no published presence at all. The programme that asks least of your money asks most of your time, and the one that asks most of your money asks nothing of your time — which is the opposite of how both are sold.

**Global Residence Programme.** **Tier B, and dated.** The tax administration's published guidelines are **Version 2.0 of 2020**, and a search of its own legislative-developments updates found no amendment to these rules. Figures as published:

- qualifying property: purchase **€275,000**, or **€220,000** in the south of Malta or Gozo; rent **€9,600** a year, or **€8,750** in the south or Gozo;
- **15%** on foreign income remitted to Malta;
- **minimum tax €15,000** a year, covering the beneficiary and qualifying dependants;
- administrative fee **€6,000**, or **€5,500** where the property is in the south;
- status ceases on "Stays in any other jurisdiction for more than 183 days in a calendar year".

**Do not publish these as current without a further check.** A six-year-old guideline is exactly the artefact this site audits other people for quoting. Note also that the GRP still carries the south-of-Malta and Gozo discount that the MPRP **abolished** — that may be a real difference between two programmes, or it may be a stale document, and the two are not distinguishable from what is published. §7.

## 7. Unresolved

1. **Whether S.L. 217.26 imposes any minimum presence.** Five registers walked (§3); the operative text is not machine-readable anywhere it is published. Needs a copy of the regulations that can be read.
2. **The date and gazette reference of L.N. 159 of 2025.** Komunità Malta names the instrument; its date was not established. A search result places a mention in the Government Gazette of 5 August 2025, which was not read.
3. **Whether the GRP thresholds in §6 are current.** The only published guidelines are from 2020.
4. **Whether the south-of-Malta and Gozo discount survives under the GRP** now that the MPRP has dropped it, or whether the 2020 document is simply stale.
5. **The agents' handbook section numbering.** §2.7 is from v2.1 of January 2024; the project cites v4.0 of January 2025 elsewhere. The annual-compliance obligation must be re-located in v4.0 before it is quoted with a section number.
6. **Whether MPRP time counts toward long-term resident status** under S.L. 217.05. Identità's page states five years of legal and continuous residence and does not address which permits qualify.
7. **The fee actually charged on the merit route.** Not published in any form.
8. **Rejection and due-diligence outcome rates for the MPRP.** Not looked for this session; the agency published none in what was walked.
9. **Whether the Court's judgment in C-181/23 has any continuing effect on the merit route**, given that the route runs on the amended regulations of the scheme the Court condemned rather than on new ones. This is a question about the judgment's reasoning and needs the judgment read, not a summary of it.
