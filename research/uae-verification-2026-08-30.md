# The UAE: what was checked, where it was read, and what could not be established

**Checked:** 30 August 2026
**Scope:** the Emirati relocation guide — the golden residence, Dubai's two-year property permit, the absence rule, what an individual actually pays, tax residency, and the treaties with Russia and Poland.
**Companion documents:** `greece-verification-2026-08-28.md`, `portugal-verification-2026-08-28.md`, `costofliving-verification-2026-08-28.md`.

## 0. How to read the tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | The operative legal text, read directly. |
| B | A government or authority page explaining its own rules. |
| C | A law firm, Big Four publication, trade press or news outlet. |
| D | Could not be established. |
| E | Asserted only by parties who sell the thing. Not usable. |

**One thing is different here from the Greek and Portuguese documents, and it is an improvement.** For Greece we could read no page of the Government Gazette and every statutory text came from a commercial reproduction. For the UAE the instruments are on the government's own legislation portal, in Arabic and English, and they were read there. Where a finding still rests on a ministry service page rather than an instrument, this document says so at the point of use.

## 1. Corrections to this project's own published statements

**1.1 The off-plan row on the sources page was wrong, and it was wrong because it read the wrong permit's page.**

It said: "No official page says off-plan property qualifies for the golden visa, and GDRFA's owner-visa page expressly requires completed construction."

The Annex to Cabinet Resolution 65/2022, article 8, Second, has a limb for exactly this:

> "The Real Estate Investor shall, when buying one or more Real Estate units **off the map** with a total value of not less than (AED 2,000,000) two million dirhams, on condition that the purchase is made from local companies determined by the Competent Local Authority."

"Off the map" (عبر الخارطة) is the standard Emirati rendering of off-plan. **Tier A.**

The "entirely constructed" wording is real, but it sits on GDRFA's page for **renewing an ordinary property-owner permit** — a different permit from the golden residence, whose own GDRFA service page states no completion requirement at all. The row had joined two documents about two products.

**What replaces it is better than a retraction.** The federal instrument admits off-plan; Dubai's own golden visa e-service asks for a title deed, and an off-plan Oqood registration is not a title deed. So the federal text permits something the emirate channel has no document for. That gap is not published anywhere we could find, on either side of the argument.

**1.2 The AED 750,000 row was right and incomplete, which on that page is its own kind of wrong.**

It said the figure "is not found in any current source", which leaves a reader to conclude it was invented. It was not. AED 750,000 was Dubai's floor for a **different permit** — the two-year property investor visa — and it was **removed in April 2026**. A sole owner now faces no minimum value at all; a co-owner's share must reach AED 400,000. **Tier B** (DLD's own page, last updated 19 August 2026), corroborated **tier C** (Fragomen, 8 May 2026).

So a page advertising a golden visa "from AED 750,000" is wrong twice over: wrong permit, and a threshold that has been repealed.

**1.3 A correction to an internal research brief, recorded because it would otherwise have reached the page.** The brief asserted that individual emirates have dormant income tax decrees that could in theory reach a resident's salary. The decrees exist and are dormant, but by their own terms they are addressed to "a body corporate wherever incorporated, or each and every branch thereof". They were never addressed to natural persons. The common formulation — "your income could theoretically be taxed under a sleeping decree" — is wrong about the addressee, not merely about enforcement. **Tier C** on the scope, which is a paraphrase; see §8.

**1.4 Three precision corrections to a first-pass reading of the Annex**, caught on the second pass and worth recording because each would have been a misquotation:

- the qualifying banks are those "**determined by** the Competent Local Authority", not "approved by";
- the health-insurance condition has a tail: "throughout the validity term of the Residence Permit **or in accordance with the terms set by the Competent Local Authority**";
- the survival of dependants' permits on the sponsor's death is **conditional** — "in accordance with the conditions determined by the ICA" — not unqualified.

**1.5 A URL we were about to publish returns 404.** The Annex was reachable at `/en/legislations/1601/regulations/836/download` when first read and is not now. The live address is `/ar/legislations/1601/regulations/1521/download`. Checked the same day. A dead citation on this site is worse than none.

## 2. The instruments, and the amendment audit

**Federal Decree-Law No. 29 of 2021** on Entry and Residence of Foreigners. Issued 16 September 2021, in force 26 October 2021, Official Gazette 712. **Tier A.**

It **never uses the words "Golden Residence"** and sets no threshold. Article 7(2): "The Executive Regulations of this Decree-Law determines the types of visas in the State." Article 8(2) does the same for residence permits; article 36 gives the power to the Council of Ministers. **Anyone citing this decree-law for the AED 2,000,000 figure is citing the wrong instrument**, and that includes several pages that cite an instrument at all.

**Cabinet Resolution No. 65 of 2022**, the Executive Regulation, with the Golden Residence in a 24-article Annex referenced by article 77. Issued 12 July 2022, in force 3 October 2022, Official Gazette 731. **Tier A.**

### 2.1 The six amendments, enumerated

The portal records six amendments and renders the list through a script, so a first pass could not read them. They were recovered through the portal's own endpoint and **each amending instrument was then read individually**.

| Instrument | Date | What it touches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cabinet Resolution 87/2022 | 2 Sep 2022 | Art. 80 — commencement moved to 3 October 2022 |
| Cabinet Resolution 117/2023 | 20 Nov 2023 | Art. 3 — adds two sea ports and a land port |
| Cabinet Resolution 95/2024 | 2 Sep 2024 | Art. 3 — a land port; **adds art. 77 bis**, Blue Residence |
| Cabinet Resolution 125/2024 | 16 Dec 2024 | Art. 3 — a sea port |
| Cabinet Resolution 179/2025 | 27 Nov 2025 | Art. 3 — a land port; **adds art. 19 bis**, a two-year work-mission visit visa |
| Cabinet Resolution 95/2026 | 3 Jun 2026 | Art. 3 — a sea port and a land port |

**Not one of the six touches article 59, article 60, or the Golden Residence Annex.** Five are border-post housekeeping. So every provision quoted in this document is the current consolidated text, verified against the complete change-set — the strong claim, not the weak one.

**The portal's own amendment log is incomplete.** Its entry for Resolution 95/2024 shows the article 3 change and omits the insertion of article 77 bis, which was found only by reading that resolution's own text. The panel cannot be relied on alone. Worth recording as a fact about the source rather than as a complaint.

**The Blue Residence annex is referenced by article 77 bis and is not published on the portal.** The regulations page lists one annex only, the Golden one. That is a publication gap in the government's own record.

### 2.2 The provisions

**Annex art. 1** — ten years, renewable, and: "allowing them to **self-reside without the need for the Guarantor/the Host Party** inside the State for work, investment, business start-up or stay." So "no sponsor" is accurate and traceable, and by its own words it extends to working, investing and starting a business.

**Annex art. 6** — seven categories and no more: investors; entrepreneurs; brilliant talented persons; scientists and specialists; excellent students and graduates; pioneers of humanitarian work; the first line of defence. Plus a discretionary limb for prominent personalities by resolution of the ICA Chairman.

**This matters for how the 2024–2026 "new categories" are described.** Nurses, teachers, e-sports professionals, content creators, waqf donors: none of these amended the Annex, because the Annex has never been amended. They are nomination programmes under art. 2's "recommendation/nomination by the Competent Federal or Local Government Authorities", slotting into headings that already existed. Presenting them as changes to the law would be wrong, and several trade-press alerts do exactly that. **Tier A** on the Annex; **tier C** on the programmes.

**Annex art. 8, Second** — the property route: AED 2,000,000; "one or more Real Estate"; the off-plan limb; the loan limb, restricted to local banks determined by the competent local authority; comprehensive health insurance. The two limbs are alternatives, joined by أو.

**The AED 2,000,000 threshold has never been amended.** The Annex carries a single date, 12 July 2022, no prior-version marker, and none of the six resolutions reaches it. **Tier A**, and this is a definite negative rather than an absence of evidence.

**Annex art. 3** — family: spouse, children "**regardless of age**", and parents, for a renewable ten years. The ordinary Emirati rule (sons to 25, daughters until married) does not apply. Domestic staff "in accordance with his financial solvency", with no numeric cap. A six-month multiple-entry entry visa, renewable once, to complete the procedures from abroad.

**No income requirement to sponsor.** Art. 4 imposes only the ability "to support himself and his family without the need for government support" and valid health insurance. Every specific salary figure found for golden visa dependants came from commercial sponsorship services. **Tier E, not usable.**

**Annex art. 5** — renewal "in accordance with the same standards and conditions under which it is granted", with ICA approval. Not automatic: at year ten the AED 2,000,000 must still be held.

**Whether the property must be held throughout is answered at emirate level, not federal.** The Annex says nothing about disposal. GDRFA Dubai's golden residence service page does: "It is required to place a lien on the property to ensure the continuity of ownership throughout the validity of the Golden Residency." **Tier B.** The mechanism is a registered restriction, not a penalty.

## 3. The absence rule

This is the item the market states most confidently and sources least.

**Main text art. 59, verbatim** (Arabic): «يعتبر تصريح الإقامة الممنوح للأجنبي لاغياً إذا تجاوزت مدة الإقامة خارج الدولة أكثر من (180) مائة وثمانين يوماً متتالياً.» — a residence permit is void if residence outside the State exceeds 180 consecutive days. **Tier A**, confirmed current.

**Main text art. 60** lists eleven exempt categories. **The words "Golden Residence" do not appear in it.** Item 9 reads «المستثمرون الحاصلون على تصاريح إقامة سارية المفعول» — "Investors holding valid Residence Permits". Item 11 is a residual power for the Chairman. **Tier A**, confirmed current.

So the legal position, precisely:

- A property-investor golden visa holder is exempt — but **through art. 60(9), a category defined by being an investor, not by holding a golden visa**. Solid, and indirect.
- A talent, student, humanitarian or frontline golden visa holder is named by no item and would depend on art. 60(11). **No such resolution is published.** **Tier D.**
- **Nothing anywhere says "indefinitely."** The instrument does not address an outer limit; it simply does not reach the question.

**And the government's own page carries a carve-out nobody publishes.** ICP's service page states that "Golden, Green, and Blue Residence holders … may enter the UAE anytime as long as the residence remains valid" — and, on the same page, "**Does not apply to Dubai residents.**" **Tier B.** Not one competitor page checked mentions it.

u.ae's golden visa page is more careful than the market: holders get "the ability to stay outside the UAE for more than the usual period of six months needed to keep their residence visa valid". **"More than six months" is not "indefinitely."**

## 4. Dubai's two-year property permit, and the 2026 changes with no instrument

**The permit.** DLD's Investor Residence application (Taskeen), page last updated 19 August 2026: "The property owner is allowed to apply for the issuance of a licence and residency visa, **regardless of the property value**", and a co-owner qualifies "provided that their share value is not less than **AED 400,000**". Two years for the investor and family; parents one year. Published fees: investor visa AED 10,212.50; family AED 6,482.25–7,382.25 per member; parents AED 8,882.25; a one-off AED 318.75 to open the sponsorship file. **Tier B.**

**The changes of 2026, and the finding that matters.** Two changes are reported everywhere: the removal of the AED 750,000 floor, around April 2026, and the removal of a 50% upfront-payment requirement for the property golden visa, around February 2026.

**No published instrument exists for either, and the origin source concedes it.** Gulf News — the report that KPMG's flash alert and LexisNexis both cite onward — states: "**While no formal announcement has been made, the updates appeared on the Cube Center, an entity affiliated with the Dubai Land Department.**"

The negative was tested rather than assumed:

- Dubai Legislation portal, stamped last updated 30 August 2026: all 32 instruments for 2026 reviewed; keyword "golden residence" returns **zero**; "residence visa" across all years returns five, none relevant.
- Memorandum Browser, classification "Residency and Foreigners Affairs": **zero**. No GDRFA–DLD memorandum of 24 April 2026 is published.
- DLD's own legislation page carries no 2025–2026 instrument on the subject.
- Secondary sources on the 50% rule all reference "a policy circular dated 20 February 2026" and **none gives a number, a reference or a link**.

**The accurate claim is "not published", not "does not exist"** — an internal circular never submitted for publication would not appear in any of those registers. That distinction goes on the page.

Note also that the 50% rule was never in the federal Annex; it was GDRFA and DLD practice, so its removal would not require an amending instrument at all.

**One unreconciled item.** GDRFA's property-owner permit renewal page states conditions that do not match DLD's Taskeen page: full ownership "or at least 2 million Dirhams of the property's value must have been paid", monthly income "at least 15,000 Dirhams", property "entirely constructed". Whether that page is stale or describes a distinct GDRFA-issued permit is **unresolved**.

**Fees do not reconcile across channels either**, and the honest treatment is to say so: GDRFA publishes AED 1,100 plus ancillaries for the golden residence permit; DLD's golden visa e-service totals AED 9,884.75; DLD's two-year investor visa is AED 10,212.50. These are plausibly three different products and channels. The composition of the AED 9,884.75 was not verified.

## 5. What an individual actually pays

### 5.1 No personal income tax — and the basis is an absence

**There is no law imposing personal income tax. There is no law granting an exemption from one.** Print the first; the second is false. u.ae states "The UAE does not levy income tax on individuals" and cites no instrument for it, which is consistent with there being none to cite. **Tier B.**

The same reasoning carries capital gains, inheritance and wealth: untaxed because no charging provision brings them into charge, not by a rule that exempts them. **Tier C** (PwC) for the enumeration.

*An adjacent trap, flagged rather than asserted: "no inheritance tax" is a statement about tax. It says nothing about succession — which law governs an estate is a separate regime, and readers conflate the two constantly. Not researched, not cited.*

### 5.2 Tax residency — Cabinet Decision 85/2022 and Ministerial Decision 27/2023

**Cabinet Decision No. 85 of 2022, art. 4**, in force 1 March 2023. **Tier A.** A natural person is a UAE tax resident if **any one** of:

1. "his usual or main place of residence and the centre of his financial and personal interests are in the State";
2. physical presence of **183 days or more** in any twelve consecutive months;
3. physical presence of **90 days or more** in any twelve consecutive months **and** UAE nationality, a valid UAE residence permit, or GCC nationality, **and additionally** either a permanent place of residence in the State **or** employment or business in the State.

**The 90-day test stacks three requirements.** Pages that render it as "90 days if you have a residence visa" have dropped a limb, and one Russian page renders it as available to property owners, which is a different error again — property ownership is not in the test.

**Ministerial Decision No. 27 of 2023, art. 3**, in force 1 March 2023. **Tier A.** "All days or parts of a day on which a natural person is physically present in the State count" — a part-day is a whole day, arrival and departure both count — and the days "do not need to be consecutive".

**Art. 4, exceptional circumstances, is discretionary and is widely reported as mandatory.** The decision says such a day "**may be disregarded by the Authority**". A Tier C summary of the FTA's own guide renders the same rule as "shall not be counted". The decision is the more authoritative text and it confers a discretion. Do not tell a reader those days are automatically excluded.

### 5.3 The certificate, and the distinction that matters

Issued by the Federal Tax Authority through EmaraTax. Submission AED 50; electronic certificate AED 500 for a registrant with a corporate tax TRN, **AED 1,000 for a natural person without one**, AED 1,750 for a juridical person without one; printed copy AED 250. Ten business days. **Tier B.**

**There are two certificates and they are not interchangeable.** One is issued on the Cabinet Decision 85/2022 criteria for domestic purposes. The other references a specific double tax agreement and requires satisfying **that treaty's own residence article**, which may test something different.

**The consequence for a reader:** a person who qualifies on the **90-day** domestic test can hold a valid UAE certificate and still be refused treaty benefits abroad, because the foreign treaty partner applies its own treaty's rules. A domestic certificate is not a shield against a Russian or Polish residency assertion.

### 5.4 Corporate tax where it touches an individual

**The AED 375,000 band is set by Cabinet Decision No. 116 of 2022, art. 2(1)** — not by Federal Decree-Law 47 of 2022, which is what nearly every page cites. **Tier A.**

**Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023, art. 2**, in force 1 June 2023. **Tier A.** A natural person's business is within corporate tax only above **AED 1,000,000** of turnover in a calendar year. And three sources are outside it "**regardless of the amount of Turnover**": wage, personal investment income, and real estate investment income.

**Rental income is outside corporate tax — but the test is licensing, not amount.** The definition: "Any investment activity conducted by a natural person related to … the sale, leasing, sub-leasing, and renting of land or real estate property in the State **that is not conducted, or does not require to be conducted through a Licence from a Licensing Authority**."

So an individual letting his own apartment on an ordinary tenancy is outside, whatever the rent. The moment the activity requires a licence, the exclusion falls away and the AED 1,000,000 test engages.

**Unresolved, and directly material.** Whether a short-term or holiday let — which in Dubai requires a DTCM holiday-home permit — falls outside the exclusion *because* it requires a licence. That is precisely the question a reader buying to let on a nightly platform needs answered, and it is **tier D**. The guide asks it and says it does not know.

### 5.5 The taxes that do exist

- **VAT 5%**, Federal Decree-Law 8/2017 art. 3. The first supply of a residential building within three years of completion is zero-rated (art. 45(9)); other residential supplies and bare land are exempt (art. 46(2), 46(3)). **Tier A-text but through an unofficial translation** — see §8.
- **The 4% Dubai transfer fee**, Executive Council Resolution 30/2013, schedule item 1, read on the Dubai Legislation portal. **Tier A.** And art. 3: "unless agreed otherwise, the Fee for the sale of Real Property will be shared **equally by the seller and purchaser**." The buyer paying all of it is a negotiated allocation under the "unless agreed otherwise" limb, not what the instrument prescribes.
- **The 5% Dubai municipality housing fee**, charged on rental value and collected through the DEWA bill. The rate and mechanism are well attested; **the instrument is not**. The best attribution found is Dubai Municipality's Director General quoted in a newspaper citing the "Dubai Municipality Revenue Law of 1962", whose text is not on the legislation portal. **Tier D on the instrument.** On this site the honest move is to publish the rate and say plainly that this one figure does not trace.
- **Social security** at 20% applies to **UAE nationals only**. Expatriates are outside it. **Tier C.**

## 6. The treaties

### 6.1 Russia — in force, and applying

Signed 17 February 2025 in Abu Dhabi; Russian ratification by Federal Law 189-FZ of 7 July 2025; **entered into force 18 July 2025**; **applies from 1 January 2026**. Dividends, interest and royalties at 10%. It replaces the limited 2011 treaty, which covered only sovereign and state financial institutions — so this is the first UAE–Russia treaty that reaches private individuals and businesses at all. **Tier C, multiply corroborated.**

Most Russian-language commentary is frozen at the February–March 2025 "expected to enter into force" stage. It is in force and has been applying for eight months.

**Not read:** the treaty text itself. A secondary source reports a 183-day employment rule and that the Protocol treats remote work as employment where the employer is resident. That second point is unusual and consequential and is **tier C**; it must not be published without reading the article.

### 6.2 Poland — the restriction is real and the conclusion drawn from it is not

**Article 4(1)(b)(i), verbatim:** a resident of the UAE means "an individual who has his domicile in the United Arab Emirates **and is a national of the United Arab Emirates**". **Tier A**, read in the Ministry of Finance's published synthesised text in both Polish and English.

So a Polish citizen on a golden visa is outside the treaty's personal scope. Polish advisers state this correctly.

**Three things they do not state, and each is checkable.**

**It was not in the 1993 treaty.** The original article 4(1) was the plain symmetrical OECD formula with no nationality element. The gate was introduced by **article 2 of the Protocol of 11 December 2013** (Dz.U. 2015 poz. 312), in force 1 May 2015, applying from 1 January 2016. **Tier A** on the 1993 text and the Protocol's wording. For twenty-two years a Pole in the Emirates was a treaty resident on ordinary criteria.

**"The treaty does not cover you" is not "you are a Polish tax resident."** Polish residence is decided by art. 3 ust. 1a of the PIT Act — centre of vital interests, or more than 183 days — and neither limb mentions nationality. Art. 4a provides that art. 3 is applied *with regard to* treaties; a treaty can displace a domestic finding, it cannot create one. **Tier A.**

**The tax authority has held exactly that.** Interpretation of the Director of KIS, 0114-KDIP3-2.4011.938.2023.1.JK3 of 27 December 2023: a Polish citizen who moved to the UAE, expressly recorded as *not* an Emirati national and holding no UAE tax certificate, was held to have **limited** Polish liability — tax on Polish-source income only. **Tier B.**

**And the provenance of the headline is worth publishing.** The much-cited line "Bez obywatelstwa Emiratów rezydencja Dubaju nie chroni przed opodatkowaniem w Polsce" is not editorial reporting by the Polish Press Agency. It is a sponsored press-office item placed by a law firm. That firm's own long-form version describes the damage as withholding rates — 19% against 5–15% on dividends — rather than as worldwide taxation. The firm contradicts its own headline.

**What is genuinely lost** for someone outside the treaty: no tie-breaker in art. 4(3), which engages only for a person resident in both States under paragraph 1; no reduced source-state rates on Polish dividends, interest or royalties; no usable certificate of residence for a Polish payer.

**Exit tax**, PIT Act arts. 30da–30db. **Tier A.** 19% of the base, or 3% where no tax base value is established. Personal assets caught are limited to partnership rights, shares, securities, derivatives and fund units. It applies only if the person was Polish-resident for **at least five of the preceding ten years**, and only above **PLN 4,000,000** of aggregate market value, that threshold applying jointly to spouses. **Instalment relief under art. 30de is available only on a move to an EU or EEA state**, so it is not available for the UAE.

**The MLI's principal purpose test is not what stops the individual**, and saying it is would misdescribe the mechanism: the PPT denies treaty benefits, and article 4(1)(b)(i) has already excluded the person from having any. The PPT bites a layer up, on arrangements through a UAE company that does satisfy 4(1)(b)(ii).

*One structural oddity worth a sentence: 4(1)(b)(ii) tests a UAE company's ownership by reference to individuals **resident** in the UAE, while 4(1)(b)(i) requires **citizenship** of the individual. The instrument is asymmetric on the same page.*

## 8. Everything this document could not establish

1. **The instrument behind either 2026 Dubai change.** Not published in any register searched. The accurate claim is "not published", not "does not exist".
2. **The Blue Residence annex**, referenced by art. 77 bis and absent from the portal.
3. **Whether a licensed short-term let falls inside or outside the real estate investment exclusion** in Cabinet Decision 49/2023. Directly material to a reader buying to let.
4. **The instrument setting the 5% Dubai municipality housing fee.** Rate attested, instrument not.
5. **Instruments for the tourism and hotel charges.** u.ae publishes rates and names no legislation.
6. **The FTA's own guides TPGTR1 and CTGREI1** could not be read directly; anything attributed to them is tier C through professional summaries.
7. **The text and scope of the emirate income tax decrees.** The "body corporate" scope is a paraphrase; the 1969 Dubai and 1965 Abu Dhabi decrees were not read. Whether any could by its terms reach a natural person trading in his own name is unverified — and that is the crux of §1.3.
8. **The VAT law's amendment history.** Article numbering was verified against an unofficial translation only, and Federal Decree-Law 18/2022 amendments were not traced article by article.
9. **Emirates ID validity for golden visa holders.** The widely repeated ten years appears only on commercial sites. **Do not publish a figure.**
10. **Whether the medical fitness test applies to golden visa holders specifically.** No official page states it either way; only the general rule for those aged 18 and over.
11. **Art. 60(11) resolutions**, which are the only possible basis for exempting non-investor golden visa categories from the 180-day rule. Unpublished.
12. **The Russia treaty's employment and remote-work articles.** Reported at tier C; the treaty text was not read.
13. **The Polish Dziennik Ustaw originals.** The Polish wording comes from the Ministry of Finance's synthesised text — official, but a consolidation. Dz.U. 2015 poz. 312 and Dz.U. 1994 nr 81 poz. 373 should be checked character by character before the Polish version is published.
14. **No Polish court authority** on art. 4(1)(b)(i). The position rests on individual interpretations that bind only their addressees.
15. **The gazette issue numbers** for five of the six amending resolutions.
16. **The composition of the AED 9,884.75 golden visa fee**, and why three government channels publish three different totals.
