## Article

When foreigners in Poland talk about "permanent residence" or "PR", they usually mean one of **two different permits**. Both are indefinite and both give free access to the labour market, but they are granted on different grounds.

The **permanent residence permit** (*zezwolenie na pobyt stały*) is granted on special grounds: Polish descent, the Card of the Pole (*Karta Polaka*), marriage to a Polish citizen, certain cases of work and international protection. The **EU long-term resident permit** (*zezwolenie na pobyt rezydenta długoterminowego UE*) comes after **5 years** of lawful stay in Poland, with income above a threshold, Polish at **B1** level and a legal title to housing.

If you have no Polish roots, no Card of the Pole and no Polish spouse — which is the position of most people who came to Poland on a work-based permit — your route to permanent residence is almost always EU long-term resident status. Below are both permits by article of the Act on Foreigners: the conditions, absences, applying through MOS, processing times, the cost and how each can be lost. Links lead to the [sources page](/sources#pl-legal), with the text of each act and the date we read it. Every route to legal residence and what to do at each stage is on the overview page, [Legalisation in Poland](entry:poland-legalisation-pillar).

### Permanent residence in Poland in 2026: the short version

- **Two permits.** Permanent residence — on special grounds; EU long-term resident — after 5 years of stay.
- **EU long-term resident.** 5 years of lawful, continuous stay, income above the threshold for the last 3 years, health insurance, Polish at B1, a legal title to housing.
- **Absences.** No single trip longer than 6 months, and no more than 10 months in total over the period that counts.
- **Applying.** Since 27 April 2026 — only through MOS, followed by an appointment for fingerprints.
- **Processing time.** 6 months by law, but at the voivode's office time limits are suspended until 4 March 2027.
- **Cost.** 640 zł stamp duty for either permit, with exemptions for certain groups.
- **The card.** The permits are indefinite; the residence card is valid for 10 years for permanent residence and 5 years for EU long-term residence.
- **Property.** 5 years after either of these permits is granted, a foreigner no longer needs the Interior Ministry (MSWiA) permit to buy a house or land.

### Permanent residence card vs EU long-term residence card in Poland: the difference

| | Permanent residence | EU long-term resident |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounds | Special cases in art. 195 | 5 years of lawful stay, art. 211 |
| Income | Only for some grounds | Above the threshold for the last 3 years |
| Polish language | Not required | B1, a Polish school or university |
| Housing | Not required | A current legal title, art. 219a |
| Health insurance | Only for some grounds | Required |
| Validity of the permit | Indefinite | Indefinite |
| Validity of the card | 10 years, art. 243 | 5 years, art. 243 |
| Work | Free access to the labour market | Free access to the labour market |
| Lost by leaving | More than 6 years outside Poland | More than 6 years outside Poland, or 12 consecutive months outside the EU |
| Stamp duty | 640 zł, with exemptions | 640 zł |

The two permits do not stack: **permanent residence expires on the day EU long-term resident status is granted**, art. 200, and you cannot apply for permanent residence once you already hold long-term resident status, art. 196. So the choice is made once.

Free access to the labour market for both is in art. 3(1)(6) and (7) of the [Act on the conditions for entrusting work to foreigners](/sources#pl-legal-praca-cudzoziemcow).

### Permanent residence permit in Poland: who is eligible

Permanent residence is granted indefinitely, on application, if one of the grounds in art. 195 of the [Act on Foreigners](/sources#pl-legal-cudzoziemcy) is met:

| Grounds | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Polish descent | Intention to settle in Poland permanently; descent is established under the rules of the Repatriation Act |
| Card of the Pole | A valid Card of the Pole and the intention to settle |
| Marriage to a Polish citizen | Married for at least 3 years before applying, and immediately before applying at least 2 years without a break on a temporary residence permit (*zezwolenie na pobyt czasowy*) granted in connection with that marriage |
| Child | A child of a Polish citizen under that parent's parental authority; a child of a holder of permanent residence or EU long-term resident status under that parent's authority, born after the permit was granted or while the parent was staying lawfully on a temporary residence permit or protection |
| Work in a sought-after occupation | 4 years without a break on a temporary residence and work permit in an occupation sought after by the Polish economy (art. 114(1a)), and a stable income |
| International protection | 5 years with refugee status, subsidiary protection or a humanitarian residence permit; 10 years on a tolerated stay permit; asylum granted |
| Other | Victims of human trafficking, subject to three conditions; certain UK citizens |

**No Polish language exam is needed for permanent residence**: art. 195 contains no language requirement. The exam is needed for EU long-term resident status and for citizenship.

**Marriage carries a trap.** If permanent residence was granted on the basis of marriage, it can be revoked if the couple divorces within 2 years of the grant, art. 199(3).

### Permanent residence in Poland for foreigners without Polish roots

Whether permanent residence is possible without Polish roots is a common question, and the honest answer is short. Without Polish descent and without a Card of the Pole, permanent residence remains open in three cases: marriage to a Polish citizen, 4 years of work in an occupation sought after by the Polish economy, and international protection.

For everyone else — people working on an ordinary temporary residence and work permit, business owners, families — the law provides **EU long-term resident status** after 5 years. When a consultant promises "permanent residence" without one of the grounds in art. 195, what is meant is almost always the long-term resident card.

### EU long-term residence permit in Poland: five requirements

EU long-term resident status is granted indefinitely, art. 211, if all of the following are met at the same time:

| Requirement | What the law requires |
| --- | --- |
| 5 years in Poland | Lawful and continuous, immediately before the application |
| Income | Stable and regular, above the social assistance threshold for you and each dependant, for the last 3 years |
| Insurance | Health insurance in Poland, or confirmation that an insurer covers treatment costs |
| Polish language | A certificate at B1 or higher, a Polish school certificate (other than post-secondary) or a university degree taught in Polish; children under 16 are exempt |
| Housing | A current legal title to the home where you will live |

**The income threshold** is the eligibility threshold for social assistance, art. 140(2), set by a [Council of Ministers regulation](/sources#pl-legal-kryteria-dochodowe) from 1 January 2025:

| Household | Monthly income must be above |
| --- | --- |
| Single applicant | 1010 zł |
| Per person in a family | 823 zł |

The threshold is reviewed every three years, so check it again before you apply.

**Housing.** Ownership, a lease or another right to use the property all qualify. A free-use agreement (*umowa użyczenia*) is accepted **only if the home is provided by your children, parents, spouse, spouse's parents or siblings**, art. 219a.

### Which periods count towards the 5 years for EU long-term residence

Counted, art. 212(1):

- **in full** — lawful stay in Poland on temporary residence permits for work, business or family;
- **at half** — university study or vocational training on the corresponding visa or permit;
- **in full if longer than 18 months**, otherwise at half — the time an international protection procedure took;
- under separate rules — periods on an EU Blue Card in other EU countries.

Not counted, art. 212(2): stays as a posted worker, an intra-corporate transfer, local border traffic, a protection procedure that ended in refusal, and certain other cases.

**If you are a citizen of Ukraine: UKR status.** You cannot apply for EU long-term resident status while on temporary protection, art. 213. Art. 212 does not say expressly whether past time with UKR status counts towards the 5 years; the voivodeship offices in Gdańsk and Poznań state that it does not, and time on a CUKR card counts from the day you receive it.

### Absences from Poland: how long you can be away

Your stay counts as continuous if **no single absence was longer than 6 months** and **all absences together did not exceed 10 months** in the period that counts. The rule is the same for permanent residence and EU long-term residence, art. 195(4) and art. 212(3).

An absence is not counted against these limits if it was due to:

- work abroad under a contract with an employer based in Poland;
- accompanying such a worker, as a spouse or a minor child;
- a special personal situation requiring presence abroad — for no longer than 6 months;
- a placement or classes abroad as part of study at a Polish university.

Count your trips before you apply: the application must list your journeys and stays abroad over the 5 years, art. 219.

### How to apply for permanent residence or EU long-term residence through MOS

MOS is the Office for Foreigners' online case system (*Moduł Obsługi Spraw*).

1. **Apply in time** — no later than the last day of your lawful stay, art. 202 and art. 218a.
2. **Fill in the form in MOS.** Since 27 April 2026 the application is filed electronically only, art. 203c and art. 219c.
3. **Attach** a photograph and scans of every page of a valid passport; for EU long-term residence, also a scan of your legal title to housing. Documents on income, insurance and language can be attached straight away, art. 203d and art. 219d.
4. **Sign** with a qualified electronic signature or a trusted profile (*profil zaufany*). By law, only a parent or guardian signing for a child signs by hand.
5. **Get the certificate** (*zaświadczenie*) confirming the application through MOS: while the case is pending, your stay is lawful until the final decision, art. 206.
6. **Attend when summoned** — no earlier than 7 days after the summons is delivered — with your passport, and for EU long-term residence with your title to housing, to give fingerprints and a specimen signature, art. 203e and art. 219e.
7. **Submit documents** when asked — within a deadline of at least 14 days, art. 203f and art. 219f.

**The main risk in the procedure is step 6.** If you do not attend on a properly served summons, do not give fingerprints or a signature, or do not show your passport or housing title, **the case is discontinued**, art. 203i and art. 219i. Summonses arrive through e-Doręczenia (the state electronic delivery service) or by registered letter, and a letter you do not collect is treated as delivered after 14 days.

### How long a decision takes

By law, a decision is issued within **6 months** of the latest of these events: a complete application filed, the documents submitted, or the deadline for them expired, art. 210. An appeal at second instance is decided within **90 days**.

But for cases at the voivode's office (*wojewoda*) on permanent residence and EU long-term resident status, **time limits are suspended until 4 March 2027**, art. 100d of the [Act on assistance to citizens of Ukraine](/sources#pl-legal-pomoc-ukraina). This applies to all foreigners, not only Ukrainians. A complaint about delay has no basis during this period.

### Permanent residence card in Poland: how much it costs

| Permit | Stamp duty | Exemption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent residence | 640 zł | Family members of a repatriate, people granted asylum, Card of the Pole holders intending to settle, certain UK citizens |
| EU long-term resident | 640 zł | — |

Amounts and exemptions are set by the annex to the [Stamp Duty Act](/sources#pl-legal-oplata-skarbowa). A separate fee is charged for issuing the residence card (*karta pobytu*).

### How permanent residence or EU long-term resident status is lost

| | Permanent residence, art. 199 | EU long-term resident, art. 215 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long absence | More than 6 years outside Poland | More than 6 years outside Poland, or 12 consecutive months outside the EU (24 for a Blue Card) |
| Crime | A final conviction in Poland for an intentional offence with a sentence of at least 3 years' imprisonment | A real and serious threat to security |
| False information | False information or documents in the application | Status obtained unlawfully |
| Other | Divorce within 2 years where the permit was granted on the basis of marriage | Long-term resident status in another EU country |

The permit is indefinite; the card is not. A permanent residence card is replaced every 10 years, a long-term resident card every 5 years. An expired card does not cancel the permit, but the document has to be replaced.

**For an EU long-term resident, the 12-month rule is the most dangerous one.** A year without a break outside the EU — back in India, Turkey, Ukraine or Belarus, for example — is grounds to revoke the status, however many years you have lived in Poland.

### Refusal of the permit and refusal to open a case

**A case will not be opened** if on the day you apply you are, among other things, in Poland unlawfully, outside Poland, on temporary protection, or — for EU long-term residence — in Poland to study, art. 196 and art. 213.

**The permit itself will be refused** if the conditions are not met or on security grounds. For permanent residence the law also lists a sham marriage, false information, **tax arrears** and unpaid costs of a previous removal, art. 197.

An appeal is filed within 14 days of the decision being delivered, through the voivode, to the Head of the Office for Foreigners (*Szef Urzędu do Spraw Cudzoziemców*).

### Permanent residence or EU long-term resident: which to choose

| Your situation | What fits |
| --- | --- |
| You hold a Card of the Pole or have Polish descent | Permanent residence: no income, language or 5-year requirement |
| Married to a Polish citizen for 3 years, and 2 years on a temporary permit based on the marriage | Permanent residence |
| 5 years of work or business in Poland, income above the threshold, B1 | EU long-term resident |
| No B1, but a Card of the Pole | Permanent residence |
| You often leave the EU for more than a year | Permanent residence, if you have a ground for it |
| 4 years on a permit in a sought-after occupation, plus income | Permanent residence |

Both permits lead to [Polish citizenship](entry:poland-citizenship) in the same way: recognition as a Polish citizen is possible after **3 years** of continuous stay on permanent residence or EU long-term resident status, with a stable income, a legal title to housing and Polish at B1, art. 30 of the Polish Citizenship Act. A presidential bill, print no. 1759 (*druk nr 1759*), proposes raising this period to 10 years and keeps the current wording for cases already started; as of 14 September 2026 it was still in committee.

### Frequently asked questions about permanent residence in Poland

? What is the difference between the EU long-term resident card and the permanent residence card?
Permanent residence is granted on special grounds — Polish descent, the Card of the Pole, marriage to a Polish citizen, certain cases of work and protection. EU long-term resident status comes after 5 years of lawful stay with income, B1 Polish, insurance and housing. A permanent residence card is valid for 10 years, a long-term resident card for 5.

? How many years do I need to live in Poland to get permanent residence?
For EU long-term resident status — 5 years of lawful, continuous stay immediately before applying. For permanent residence the period depends on the ground: with a Card of the Pole there is none, through marriage it is 2 years on a temporary permit based on the marriage, and through work in a sought-after occupation it is 4 years.

? What are the requirements for permanent residence in Poland?
For EU long-term residence: 5 years of lawful, continuous stay, a stable income above the social assistance threshold for the last 3 years, health insurance, Polish at B1 and a legal title to housing. For the permanent residence permit: one of the grounds in art. 195, such as Polish descent, a Card of the Pole or marriage to a Polish citizen.

? Do I need to pass a Polish exam for permanent residence?
Not for permanent residence under art. 195. For EU long-term resident status you need a Polish certificate at B1 or higher, or a Polish school or university taught in Polish.

? Can I get permanent residence in Poland without Polish roots?
Yes, in three cases: marriage to a Polish citizen, 4 years of work in an occupation sought after by the Polish economy, and international protection. Otherwise, the route is EU long-term resident status.

? How long can I be outside Poland without breaking the 5 years?
No single absence longer than 6 months and no more than 10 months in total, except trips for work with a Polish employer and a few other exceptions.

? How long can I be away with an EU long-term resident card?
The status is revoked if you leave Poland for more than 6 years or leave the EU for 12 consecutive months.

? How many years is the EU long-term resident card valid for?
The permit itself is indefinite; the card is valid for 5 years from issue and is then replaced. A permanent residence card is valid for 10 years.

? Does UKR status count towards the 5 years for EU long-term residence?
Art. 212 does not say so expressly, and the voivodeship offices in Gdańsk and Poznań state that it does not. You cannot apply for long-term residence while on temporary protection. Time on a CUKR card counts from the day you receive it.

? What income do I need for the EU long-term resident card?
Above the social assistance threshold for you and each dependant for the last 3 years: from 1 January 2025, 1010 zł for a single applicant and 823 zł per person in a family.

? Can I work with a permanent residence card or an EU long-term resident card?
Yes, both permits give free access to the labour market, without a work permit.

? How long does a permanent residence application take?
6 months by law, but at the voivode's office time limits in these cases are suspended until 4 March 2027.

? What happens if I miss the fingerprint appointment?
The case is discontinued. The appointment is set at least 7 days after the summons is delivered, and the law treats an uncollected letter as delivered after 14 days.

### When permanent residence is not a simple case

Applying for EU long-term resident status on your own is realistic if your 5 years are unbroken, your income has stayed above the threshold, you hold a B1 certificate and your housing is documented. It is a different matter if:

- the 5 years include long trips abroad or a change in the basis of your stay;
- your income fell below the threshold in any year;
- you are choosing between permanent residence and EU long-term resident status;
- the ground is marriage, and questions about whether it is genuine are likely;
- the case was discontinued for failure to attend, or a refusal has arrived.
