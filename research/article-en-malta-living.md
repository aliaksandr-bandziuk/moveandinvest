## Article

Here are five answers to the same question, all taken from the first screen of results for the cost of living in Malta, all describing one single person, all published within the last year.

€781.9 a month. €785 a month. Around $2,100 a month. €2,205 a month. And, from a page about rent alone, $1,000 to $1,500.

The spread between the lowest and the highest is a factor of 2.8. Not one of those pages says where its number came from.

This one does, and it starts with the thing that makes Malta unusual.

### Rent in Malta: the public lease register says €850, not €1,400

Since **1 January 2020**, every private residential lease in Malta has had to be registered with the **Housing Authority**. Not surveyed, not sampled — registered, as a legal duty, by the landlord.

That produces something most countries simply do not have: a record of what tenants actually signed, sitting alongside the advertisements for what landlords hoped to get. At the end of 2023 the register held **60,339 active contracts**.

![1]

The two datasets do not agree, and the size of the disagreement has been measured twice by the Central Bank of Malta.

**In its 2025 working paper**, the Bank put the median rent of a **new contract registered in 2023 at €850 a month**. Over the same market, the median asking price on Facebook Marketplace was **€1,400**, and the median in an aggregated dataset of estate-agency listings was **€1,500**.

**In an earlier 2022 study**, the Bank compared the whole distributions rather than the medians, and the result is starker still: in 2021, **51% of registered properties rented for under €700 a month**, against **under 9% of advertised ones**.

### Why the registered Maltese rent is not a discount you can ask for

It would be easy, and wrong, to conclude that Maltese rent is 40% cheaper than the internet says and to budget accordingly.

**The two datasets are not describing the same homes.** In the agency listings, **53%** of properties have three or more bedrooms; in the register, **44%** do. Part of the gap is simply that bigger flats are the ones worth advertising.

**And the register contains renewals.** The Central Bank flags this itself: a renewed contract usually carries the same rent as before, so the registered series moves more slowly than the market. Between 2020 and mid-2022 the registered figures grew 4.7% to 5.7% a year while the advertised ones grew 13.6%. Some of that is the advertisements running ahead of reality; some of it is the register looking backwards at leases signed years ago.

So the honest reading is narrower than "rent is cheaper than they say". It is this: **the number you will be quoted is an asking price for a larger-than-typical property, and the number people are actually paying, including everyone who stayed put, is a long way below it.** Which of those two you will face depends on whether you are entering the market or already in it — and someone moving to Malta is, by definition, entering it.

The register's own local figures give a feel for the level. In **St Paul's Bay**, which alone holds just under 8,900 contracts — 15% of the entire national register — the median two-bedroom went for **€750** and the median three-bedroom for **€900** in the second half of 2023. Just under half the register, **43%**, sits in the Northern Harbour region.

**One caution about dates, and we would rather lose the sale than skip it.** The newest register report we could find published covers the second half of **2023**. Presenting €850 as a 2026 figure would be exactly the error this page exists to point at. It is the newest official median for a newly signed lease; it is not this year's.

### Malta stopped measuring household spending after the 2015–2016 survey

Rent is the half of "cost of living" that Malta measures well. The other half it does not measure at all.

A national picture of what households actually spend — on food, transport, utilities, everything — comes from a **Household Budget Survey**. Malta's statistics office runs one. And on NSO's own page explaining the survey:

**The last completed Maltese Household Budget Survey covers 2015 and 2016.**

![2]

The next one was collected in the field from **November 2024 to December 2025**, over a full 52-week cycle so that seasonal spending is caught. Analysis is to be finished in the third quarter of 2026, and the results published **before the end of 2026** — which is to say, within a few months of you reading this.

Until then, there is no current official answer to what a Maltese household spends. Every monthly budget you will be quoted for Malta is resting on something else — crowd-sourced submissions, an insurer's blog, an agency's estimate — and the reason they disagree by a factor of 2.8 is that there is nothing underneath them to agree with.

That is not a gap in Maltese statistics so much as a fact about the calendar, and it is about to close. It is worth knowing which side of it you are reading on.

### What Malta does measure monthly: the harmonised index of consumer prices

Prices, as opposed to spending, are current and published monthly.

The **Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices for March 2026**, released by NSO on 16 April 2026, put annual inflation at **2.3%**, unchanged from the previous month, with a twelve-month moving average of **2.5%**.

The composition is more useful than the headline to anyone deciding whether to move:

- **Recreation, sport and culture rose 4.6%** and **restaurants and accommodation services 3.9%** — the two largest upward contributions. These are the categories a newly arrived foreign resident spends disproportionately in, which means the inflation an expatriate experiences is higher than the national figure.
- **Clothing and footwear fell 3.9%** and **information and communication fell 3.6%** — the two largest downward contributions, and both are small shares of a household budget.

A national average of 2.3% therefore understates the increase facing someone who eats out and goes out, and overstates it for someone who does not.

### What this page will not tell you about the cost of living in Malta

Three things, and each is a decision rather than an omission.

**A monthly budget figure.** We are not going to invent one, and no honest source can supply one for Malta until the survey is published. What you can do instead is build it: the rent from the register, at the level for the locality and size you actually want; the price movements from the monthly index; and the rest from your own current spending, which you know better than any survey does.

**A minimum wage.** Malta's national minimum wage is set by a National Standard Order, and the government's legislation portal did not render its text for us. Figures for 2026 are in wide circulation and we could not trace one to the instrument that sets it, so we are not printing a number we have not read.

**A purchase price.** The lease register prices renting, not buying. Nothing in it supports a statement about property prices, and this page makes none.

### Frequently asked questions about the cost of living in Malta

? How much does it cost to live in Malta per month?
There is no current official answer, and that is the honest position rather than an evasion. Malta's last completed Household Budget Survey covers 2015–2016; the next was collected between November 2024 and December 2025 and is due to be published before the end of 2026. The monthly figures in circulation — anywhere between €782 and €2,205 for one person — are not drawn from a national expenditure survey, because there has not been one for a decade.

? What is the average rent in Malta?
The median rent on a lease newly registered in 2023 was €850 a month, from the Housing Authority's register of signed contracts, as measured by the Central Bank of Malta. Advertised medians for the same year were €1,400 on Facebook Marketplace and €1,500 across estate agency listings. Part of that gap is that advertised properties are larger: 53% of agency listings have three or more bedrooms against 44% of registered homes.

? Why do rent figures for Malta differ so much between websites?
Because most of them price advertisements and one dataset prices signed contracts. Malta has required registration of private residential leases since January 2020, so both exist. In 2021, 51% of registered properties rented for under €700 a month against under 9% of advertised ones.

? Is Malta cheaper than the rest of Europe?
Comparisons you will see ranking Malta against other European cities are generally derived from crowd-sourced data rather than from national statistics, and they are not comparing like with like across countries. What can be said from official sources is that Maltese annual inflation stood at 2.3% in March 2026, with recreation and restaurants rising faster than the average.

? Is the rent register available to the public?
The Housing Authority publishes periodic rent reports drawn from it, and the Central Bank of Malta has published analyses using the underlying data. The most recent report we found covers the second half of 2023.

? Does the €850 figure include bills?
It is contractual rent. Utilities, the annual registration of the contract, agency fees where an agent is used, and the deposit are separate, and the register does not price them.
